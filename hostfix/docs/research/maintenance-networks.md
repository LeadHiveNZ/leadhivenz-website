## Approvals, vetting, call-backs

- **Spend limits:** Latchel shows vendors a soft budget and calls the owner above a hard budget (minimums US$200 and US$500). Work under budget is done on the first visit [6]. Honey Homes needs in-app approval for parts over US$100 [13].
- **Vetting:** Lula requires US$1m liability cover, a licence where the work needs one, and a criminal check [3]. Property Meld runs a five-point check that includes licence and insurance documents [10]. Builderscrack says it verifies ID, NZBN, address, insurance and licences, but its terms disclaim that members are licensed [19]. Latchel's fallback network "isn't a vetted or licensed network" [8]. I found no published expiry-tracking rules or removal thresholds.
- **Call-backs:** Lula's original pro must return; otherwise the job is reassigned and the cost is deducted from future payouts [4]. Latchel asks the vendor back, then refunds the owner [6].

## Praise and complaints

- **Owners, praise:** "Lula handled it spectacularly" (Mar 2025) [5]; "got heaps of offers really quickly" (Builderscrack, Nov 2026) [19].
- **Owners, complaints:** "the agents kept rerouting me back to the main menu and then dropping my call" (Lula, Mar 2025) [5]; "They charged me $15 connection fee for $60 job" (Airtasker, Nov 2026) [20]; "he was a 2 hour minimum so it cost me double" (TaskRabbit, Nov 2026) [23]. Early Honey Homes members reported too few technicians and uneven quality [13].
- **Tradies, praise:** "Lula pays quickly, and most importantly, consistently" (company-published) [3].
- **Tradies, complaints:** "there is no money (tokens) refunded if you don't win the job" (Builderscrack, Oct 2026) [19]; "Scam/spam and totally unqualified leads that we are forced to pay for" (Thumbtack, Nov 2026) [22]; "Long time before get paid" (Airtasker, Nov 2026) [20]; "my account was blocked for almost a month because I did not buy the subscription" (Urban Company, 2024 article) [21].

## B. What works

1. **Lula:** "the quote is the invoice". Pros pay 0% on catalog handyman jobs against 12% elsewhere, which steers supply onto the menu [1, 2].
2. **Latchel:** troubleshooting before dispatch; it claims 23% of requests are solved at intake [7]. AA says almost 70% are fixed on the first visit [16].
3. **Latchel and Turno:** preferred supplier first, then a defined fallback [6, 8, 26].
4. **Latchel:** two budget levels, so small jobs finish on the first visit [6].
5. **Latchel, Humming Homes, Lula:** fast, stated payout is the leading selling point to tradies [3, 9, 14].
6. **Lula:** no-show rules cut both ways. The pro is paid US$50 if nobody is home, and pays US$50 for a late start or a cancellation inside 24 hours [1].
7. **Lula:** call-back duty enforced through payout deductions [4].
8. **Jim's:** a pay-for-work guarantee supports franchisees when work is short [25].

## C. Where they fail

1. **Paying for leads that do not convert.** hipages Australia retains only 58% of monthly recurring revenue over 12 months [18]. Thumbtack scores 2.0 on Trustpilot [22].
2. **Lock-in.** hipages gave the regulator an enforceable undertaking in May 2023 over auto-renewal and exit fees [18]; cancellation complaints continue in 2026 (S).
3. **Undisclosed deductions and fines.** Handy agreed to a US$2.95m settlement in 2025 (S) [27]. Urban Company partners report penalties and blocked accounts [21].
4. **Fee stacking on small jobs.** See the Airtasker and TaskRabbit quotes above.
5. **Bypass.** Builderscrack's terms ban off-platform deals and referrals [19]. Urban Company partners report penalties for suspected offline jobs [21]. Platforms that only introduce never see the repeat work (I).
6. **Coordination without accountable supply.** Latchel's fallback is unvetted [8]. A property manager on Lula: "Admins answering the phones & assigning work, that just know the basics" [5].
7. **Thin supply.** Jim's leaves more than 200,000 leads a year unserviced [25]. Latchel-sourced jobs auto-cancel after about 48 hours unclaimed [6]. In a small town, a credit-backed guarantee will be tested by capacity more than by behaviour (I).
8. **Quality varies by operator.** Hire A Hubby scores 4.0 on one review site and 2 out of 5 on another (S) [24].

## D. Benchmarks

- **Take rate on job value:** 12% from the pro (Lula); up to 15% from the customer (Humming Homes); 12.5–20% from the tasker plus a customer fee (Airtasker); 28.3% (Urban Company); 2% capped at A$20 per invoice (OurProperty, S) [31]; about 6% of turnover as a flat fee (Jim's). 15% is mid-range (I).
- **Lead models:** A$2,575/yr per tradie (hipages); A$9–18 per lead (Jim's).
- **Approval limits:** US$200 soft and US$500 hard (Latchel). US$250–500 is common in US management agreements, and one NZ template uses one week's rent (S) [29]. NZ$400 is in line (I).
- **Payout:** same day (Latchel, Humming Homes); 1–3 business days (TaskRabbit); 2–5 (Turno); 3–5 after release (Airtasker); 5 (Lula). 48 hours would be near the fast end (I).
- **Subscriptions:** coordination-only US$149/yr (Lula through a landlord app, Dec 2024) [5] to US$199–599/yr (Humming Homes); emergency plans NZ$34–54/yr (AA) and A$273/yr (RACV); software US$2/unit/month (Property Meld), A$0.50–0.90 (Bricks + Agent), US$8–10 (Turno); labour-included US$2,000–5,500/yr (Honey Homes). NZ$99–149 a month with labour charged separately sits well above every coordination-only benchmark (I).

## E. Closest equivalents

- **Lula:** managed network, fixed-price catalog, commission from the pro. It differs in having no membership, selling to US property managers with 150–5,000 doors, and publishing no response guarantee.
- **Latchel:** monthly fee, preferred-then-fallback dispatch, human coordinators, budgets. The vendors are the owner's, the fallback is unvetted, and there is no menu.
- **Humming Homes:** membership plus up to 15%, with response-time promises. It serves homeowners with quoted work and states no remedy.
- **AA Home Response (NZ):** subscription, fixed first-hour price, roughly one-hour arrival, and it covers holiday homes. It is emergencies only and not guaranteed.
- **Turno:** short-stay specific, primary-then-backup, auto-pay. It covers cleaning.
- **Local and niche:** Queenstown holiday-home managers bundle maintenance into management, prices unpublished (S) [30]. The Quote Yard (AU, 2025) sells short-stay handyman leads to tradies for A$35–99/month, with no dispatch or guarantee [28].
- **Not found in anything I read:** an automatic credit for a missed response time, or a timed first-choice-then-backup offer measured in minutes.
