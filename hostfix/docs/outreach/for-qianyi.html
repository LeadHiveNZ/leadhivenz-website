What your feedback changed

Qianyi, thank you. You told me five things I hadn't thought through properly, and every one of them changed how HostFix works. This is what's changing before launch, and what it means in dollars for a sparky like your partner.

One thing first, because it shapes everything else: HostFix takes all kinds of maintenance, but Airbnb and short-stay homes are the main focus. In those homes, time matters. A light out or a dripping tap needs sorting in a day or two, not next week. That speed is what owners will be paying for.

All prices include GST. Owner prices are the Queenstown menu; the tradie's share is what lands in their bank account.

You said, so we changed

Five points, five changes

You said

Changing lights is a big part of the work, and it needs an electrician.

We changed

Light fittings are now electrician jobs. Owners report the problem ("light not working"), not the trade, and the app sends it to a sparky.

You said

Clients bundle jobs. One light on its own is expensive. That's why you went back to hourly.

We changed

One visit, several jobs. The first item is the menu price, each extra item on the same visit is half price. Jobs that can't be priced up front are hourly.

You said

Fittings vary and clients want to choose.

We changed

A quote can carry two or three product options with prices. The owner picks before any work starts.

You said

Most jobs can wait a few days, and managers use the sparky they trust.

We changed

Small jobs stay within 24 hours: between guests, that speed is the whole point. Bigger or quoted work that needs planning gets a "within 5 days" option, so it can be fitted around other jobs. And an owner's own tradie always gets first offer on their properties. If he can't make the time, he can suggest another, and the owner decides. Details below.

You said

15% won't work on big jobs, and the 12-month rule doesn't fit a small town where everyone already knows each other.

We changed

The fee drops on big jobs. A tradie's own clients are never HostFix's. And the 12-month rule is narrower, with a simple way out. Details below.

When it's the owner's own sparky

"Can't make that time" is not a no

A manager's regular sparky shouldn't have to hit "pass" just because he's booked solid tomorrow. So the tradie an owner has chosen gets three buttons, not two:

He tapsWhat the owner sees
Accept, and picks an arrival time"Dave's coming tomorrow, 9 to 11am."
Can't make that time, suggest another"Dave can't make it within 24 hours. He can come Thursday, 9 to 11am. Wait for Dave or send it to another HostFix tradie now." The owner decides. If they wait, Thursday becomes the time Dave is held to, and if the job was urgent, nobody pays the urgent fee.
Pass"Dave isn't available for this one, so we've sent it to another HostFix tradie." Never "passed" or "declined".

Neither suggesting a time nor passing ever counts against a tradie. What does count is turning up when he said he would: tradies who are on time on the jobs they accept are offered network jobs first. Urgent jobs pay the extra $42.50 when he's on site within 4 hours; late, and the owner isn't charged it.

The 12-month rule, rewritten

HostFix pays to find owners. The rule protects that, and nothing more.

  • Only clients HostFix introduced. Anyone a tradie already works with is listed at sign-up and is theirs, full stop.
  • 12 months from the first job, not the last, so it always ends.
  • A clean way out. If a tradie and an owner want to deal directly sooner, it's $250 per property, once, and they're free.
  • After 12 months, that owner is the tradie's own client, and the app is 1% if they keep using it.
