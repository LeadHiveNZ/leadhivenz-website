## 1. What Mitch actually does (plain English)

He is a **commission broker for used trucks and earthmoving gear**, not a marketplace and not a dealer. An operator with a surplus digger or truck hands it to Mitch; the machine **keeps working on contract** while Mitch lists it (Deals on Wheels, Trade Me, MarketBook, Facebook, his own site), runs viewings, gets independent inspections, negotiates and closes. He gets paid when it sells. He and his wife Nicky run it from a yard at 830 Oropi Road. He claims an average of $1.9M of gross sales a year over the last 3 years. He has 76 live listings right now, 65 of them POA.

His constraint is **supply**. Every listing he wins is inventory he can sell to a buyer pool he already has (NZ contractors plus Pacific Island buyers). His channels for finding sellers are all passive: word of mouth, "scouring the country", and classifieds that buyers look at, not sellers. There is nothing on his site asking sellers to come to him, no appraisal CTA, no intake form, no Meta Pixel, no Google Analytics. The site was last touched in 2022.

So when he wrote "lead generation surplus truck and machinery", he means: **find me business owners who have gear parked up and want it gone.** That is exactly a Meta job.

## 2. Why this is a deal worth chasing

- **High ticket.** A single $40k machine at a 5–10% commission is $2k–$4k to him. One extra listing sold a month pays for the whole retainer. The ROI story is simple and you do not need to stretch any numbers.
- **Nationwide, one client, no exclusivity headache.** New niche for LeadHive. You can offer him NZ-wide exclusivity for "truck and machinery brokerage" at no cost to you.
- **The market is handing you the hook.** 42% of civil firms cut staff last year, 73% cite lack of work, 768 construction firms liquidated in the year to March 2026, diesel spiked 85% in March, the OCR has been hiked twice. CCNZ's own CEO said contractors are "parking up equipment that is surplus to requirements". Teletrac found NZ gear sits unused about half the time. Sellers exist. Nobody is advertising to them.
- **Website upsell is natural, not bolted on.** The ads need a landing page and a pixel. His current site cannot take a seller lead properly. The website is part of the campaign build, not a separate pitch.

## 3. Risks you need to go in eyes open about

- **Volume is unproven.** This is B2B on Meta in a small country. Published CPLs for industrial and commercial are US$36 to AUD$51 median, B2B datasets up to $145. Expect NZ$40–150 per raw seller lead. Do **not** promise 15–25 leads a month. Sell a 90-day pilot measured on appraisals booked and listings won.
- **$750 of ad spend is thin for nationwide B2B.** Recommend you push the $2,000 Growth tier ($1,000+ spend). If he baulks, run Starter but set expectations at 8–15 enquiries a month, not 20+.
- **Meta is not your home turf.** Your skills and systems are Google Ads. Treat the build like a new product: pixel + Conversions API, instant forms, creative testing, lead routing. Budget your own time for it.
- **Lead quality will be messy.** Expect private sellers with a $6k ute, tyre-kickers, and people who want a free valuation. The intake form has to qualify (commercial gear, approximate value, timeframe) and Mitch has to be fine with calling 10 to win 2.
- **Turners is on his doorstep** (Hull Rd, Mt Maunganui) and Independent Equipment is the broker competitor with an appraisals CTA. He will mention them. His answer is "keep it on contract while it sells, no yard, no auction fire-sale, I do the viewings". Your answer is "they are not running seller ads either; first mover wins the feed".

## 5. The campaign plan (what you are actually selling)

**Objective:** Meta Leads campaign, NZ-wide, instant forms plus landing page, Advantage+ placements. Facebook and Instagram.

**Audiences** (check every interest live in Ads Manager; Meta retired a lot of detailed interests in Jan 2026):
1. Broad NZ, 25–65, with the creative doing the filtering. In a market this small, broad usually beats stacked interests.
2. Interest layer to test: excavators, earthmoving, civil engineering, forestry, trucking, Caterpillar, Komatsu, Hitachi, Kobelco, Volvo CE, Isuzu Trucks, Hino, Scania, Kenworth.
3. Custom audience from Mitch's past buyers and sellers (ask for his contacts list), then a 1–3% lookalike once it is seeded.
4. Retargeting: site visitors and video viewers.
Facebook groups (NZ earthmoving, diggers for sale, trucking) cannot be targeted. They are for Mitch to post in organically and for you to drop the landing page into once a week.

**Intake form fields:** name, mobile, region, machine type, make/model/year, approx hours or km, finance owing (yes/no), when do you want it sold (now / 1–3 months / just curious), photos (landing page only). The "just curious" option is deliberate; it sorts leads for Mitch before he calls.

**Six ad angles to test (two per week):**
1. *Holding cost:* "Parked up and still paying for it? Finance, insurance and depreciation do not stop when the machine does."
2. *Mitch's edge:* "Keep it earning on contract while we sell it. No yard, no auction, no downtime."
3. *Anti-auction:* "Don't fire-sale it. Turners charges 9.5% and the buyer pays 10.75% on top. We find the buyer and you set the price."
4. *Downturn:* "Downsizing the fleet? We've sold $1.9M a year of trucks and machinery on behalf of NZ operators."
5. *Export buyers:* "Buyers in NZ and across the Pacific Islands. Your machine gets seen by people who actually buy."
6. *Proof:* carousel of recently sold units with make, model and days-to-sell.

**Creative:** phone video of Mitch walking around a listed machine, 30 seconds, straight to camera, with captions. Carousels from his existing listing photos. One static per angle. No stock imagery; this audience smells it instantly.

**Lead handling:** instant SMS and email to Mitch on every form (your Make.com flow), plus a weekly sheet. Speed-to-lead target under 15 minutes in business hours. Agree up front that he calls every lead the same day; if he will not, do not run the campaign.

**KPIs for the 90-day pilot:** cost per raw lead, % qualified (commercial gear worth $15k+), appraisals booked, listings won, units sold. The number that matters to him is listings won. Report it monthly with your lead-report skill.

## 9. Three decisions (added after Joe's questions)

### 9a. Which page the ads run from: Mitch's page, your ad account

**Run the ads from the Smith Traders Facebook page, through a LeadHive-owned ad account.** Not from LeadHive's page and never from a personal profile (Meta does not allow ads from a profile anyway).

Why: the seller is trusting "Smith Traders" with a $40k machine. An ad from LeadHive NZ asking them to sell their digger converts at a fraction of the rate and looks like a lead-reseller. The page is his face. The ad account, the pixel, the creative and the lead routing are yours. That split keeps you sticky (he cannot just take the campaign and run it himself) while the ads look exactly like they should.

Setup, in order:
1. **Business Portfolio.** business.facebook.com. Use the LeadHive NZ portfolio (create it if you only have a personal ad account). Verify the business while you are there; it lifts spend limits and reduces account flags.
2. **Ad account.** Create "Smith Traders – Seller Leads" under the LeadHive portfolio. Currency NZD, time zone Pacific/Auckland, your card as the payment method. Ad spend is inside the package so billing stays with you. One client per ad account, always.
3. **Page access.** Business settings → Accounts → Pages → Add → "Request access to a Page". Enter the Smith Traders 2010 Limited page. Ask for **Create ads** and **Manage content** (so you can post the landing page in the feed too). Mitch approves it from Business Suite → Settings → Page access, or from the notification Meta sends him. He must be an admin of the page, not an editor. If someone else set the page up years ago, that is the first thing to sort on the call.
4. **Instagram.** If he has an account, link it to the page and request access the same way. If he does not, run Instagram placements anyway. Meta lets the page act as the Instagram identity.
5. **Pixel / dataset.** Create it under the LeadHive portfolio, install it on the new site, and turn on Conversions API through Make or the site platform. Do not put it under his portfolio.
6. **Instant Forms and lead access.** Forms belong to the page, so the leads are legally his, which is the right outcome. You need **Leads Access** granted to you: Business Suite → All tools → Instant Forms → Leads access (Mitch assigns you or the LeadHive business as a CRM user). Then Make.com "Facebook Lead Ads" trigger → SMS and email to Mitch → Google Sheet. Same pattern as your Nimbata flows.
7. **Privacy policy URL.** Instant Forms will not publish without one. Put a privacy page on the new site in week one.
8. **Policy check on the copy.** Meta's personal-attributes rule bans ads that imply the viewer's financial situation. "Parked up and still paying for it?" is about the machine, fine. "Downsizing the fleet?" fine. Do not run "Business going under?" or "Can't make the repayments?". That gets the account flagged.

What to tell Mitch on the call: "Ads run from your Smith Traders page, so every seller sees your name. I run them from my ad account so you never touch Ads Manager or a card. You own the page and every lead. I own the build."
