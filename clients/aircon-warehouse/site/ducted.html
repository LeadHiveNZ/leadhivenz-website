How ducted works

One system, every room

A ducted heat pump works like a high wall with the indoor part moved into the roof. All you see is a slim vent in each ceiling and a controller on the wall.

Who it suits

Is ducted right for your home?

Ducted is excellent in the right house and overkill in the wrong one. Here’s how we’d call it.

A great fit

  • New builds and renovations
  • Homes with good roof-space access
  • Open-plan living plus several bedrooms
  • Owners who want nothing on the walls

Think about high wall instead

  • You only need one or two rooms done
  • The roof space is tight or hard to reach
  • It’s a rental and budget comes first

Zoning and control

Heat the rooms you’re in, not the whole house

Zoning splits the house so living areas and bedrooms run on their own settings, all from one outdoor unit.

  • Living and sleeping zones

    Warm the lounge in the evening and the bedrooms before bed, without paying to heat empty rooms.

  • Timers that suit your day

    Have the house warm before you get up and switch off once everyone has left.

  • Wall and app control

    A slim controller on the wall, with app control on most systems so you can turn it on before you get home.

  • One outdoor unit

    One outdoor unit runs the whole system, placed with your neighbours in mind.

FAQ

Ducted questions

Still weighing it up? Call 09 215 9544, 7 days, 8am–8pm.

How long does a ducted install take?

Usually 1–3 days, depending on the size of the home, roof-space access and how many rooms are being done. We confirm timing after the site visit, along with your fixed price.

Can ducted be retrofitted into an existing home?

Often, yes. It needs enough roof space for the fan coil and duct runs, safe access to get them in, and a sensible spot for the outdoor unit. Single-storey homes with a pitched roof are usually straightforward.

We check all of this on the site visit, and we’ll tell you straight if high-wall units would suit the house better.

Does ducted cost more to run than several high walls?

Room for room, ducted uses a little more power, because some heat is lost through the ducts. Zoning narrows the gap: heat only the rooms you’re using and it stays efficient.

If you only ever use one or two rooms, high walls are usually the cheaper choice, and we’ll say so.

What maintenance does a ducted system need?

Check and clean the return-air filter monthly through winter; it takes a few minutes. Have a professional service every 12 months, or every 6 if the system works hard.

We show you where the filter is and how to clean it on install day.
