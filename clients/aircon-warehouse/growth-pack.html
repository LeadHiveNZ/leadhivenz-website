01 · What the ad sells

The trade-partner offer

The ad sells one thing: a trade account. Not “contact us”, not “learn more”.

The offer “Trade pricing on Daikin, Fujitsu (GENERAL Airstage) and Haier. Supply only or supply and install. Quotes from your plans within one business day. Open 7 days.”

Proof points

  • Warehouse in Wiri.
  • Multi-unit developments already supplied and installed (the three-storey apartment build photo).
  • One contact for the whole job.
  • 7-day availability.

Who it is for

  • Builders and developers: new builds, townhouses, apartments.
  • Property managers and body corporates: portfolios, Healthy Homes heating.
  • Electricians and plumbers who want to resell or sub-contract heat pump installs.
  • Commercial fit-out companies: offices, retail, hospitality.
What we never claim

Accreditations, warranty years, ratings, “cheapest in Auckland”, “#1”.

06 · Own your website

Hosting it in-house: the recommendation

Darren wants to own and run the website himself. The site is plain HTML, CSS and JavaScript, which is the easiest possible thing to self-host. No WordPress, no plugins, nothing to update, nothing to get hacked.

Netlify (free plan)

Recommended

Why: drag-and-drop deploys, free SSL, a global CDN so it loads fast on a phone in Manukau or Albany, and Netlify Forms, which means the quote and trade forms on the site already work the moment it’s deployed (100 submissions a month free; the forms are already tagged for it). Zero monthly cost until the site is busy enough to need the $19 plan.

Steps 30 minutes

  1. Create a free Netlify account at netlify.com with info@airconwarehouse.co.nz.
  2. Sites → Add new site → Deploy manually → drag the aircon-warehouse-netlify.zip onto the box. The site is live on a netlify.app address in about 20 seconds.
  3. Site configuration → Forms: turn Form detection on, then redeploy once. Forms & notifications → add an email notification to info@airconwarehouse.co.nz for every submission (and a second one to a phone via the “Slack/SMS” integrations if wanted).
  4. Domain management → Add a domain → airconwarehouse.co.nz. Netlify shows two DNS records. Log in to wherever the domain is registered (the old WordPress host or the registrar), replace the A/CNAME records with Netlify’s, and wait up to an hour. Turn on HTTPS (automatic).
  5. Before switching DNS, keep the old WooCommerce site reachable for a week on a sub-domain (old.airconwarehouse.co.nz) in case anything on it is still needed, and set up redirects for the old /products/ and /product-category/ URLs to the new pages (a _redirects file; Joe will supply it).

Making changes yourself

The pages are text files. Open the file in any editor (VS Code is free), change the words or prices, save, and drag the folder onto Netlify again.

Or, the in-house way Joe will show on the Zoom: paste the page into Claude, say what to change, paste the result back and redeploy. Ten minutes a change, no developer.

If Darren would rather have a visual editor, the honest alternatives are: Webflow (NZ$20–30 a month, the site would be rebuilt in it) or keeping the site on Netlify and adding a free CMS (Decap) later.

Not recommended

Moving the new site back into WordPress. It would be rebuilt as a theme, cost more than the site did, and bring back updates, plugins and hosting bills.

Also connect

  • Google Search Console (submit the sitemap).
  • A Meta Pixel.

Netlify Analytics is $9 a month if he wants traffic numbers without Google Analytics.
