01Common faults

What's actually going wrong up there.

Most spouting problems come down to a handful of faults. Here is how each one shows up, and what usually causes it.

  • Leaking joins and cornersSealant dries out and joins open up as the spouting expands and contracts. You'll see drips at the join, or a dark streak on the wall below.
  • Overflow at the stop-endWater spills from the end of a run when the stop-end seal has failed, or when the fall is sending water to the end instead of the outlet.
  • Sagging between bracketsBrackets too far apart, or a run heavy with leaves and water, makes the spouting dip. Water then sits in the low spot, rusts steel and overflows.
  • Failed bracketsBrackets rust through or pull out of tired fascia. One failure can bring a whole length down, so we check the fascia at the same time.
  • Wrong fallSpouting needs a gentle slope towards the downpipe. If water stands in it after rain, the fall is off, often from a sagging bracket or an old install.
  • Blocked outletsLeaves, moss and roof grit clog the outlet, the spouting fills and water pours over the front and down the wall.
  • Cracked uPVCSun and age make uPVC brittle. A ladder knock, a branch or just time can split a length or a joiner, and those parts can often be swapped out.
  • Rust holes in steelOlder steel and galvanised spouting rusts from the inside, where water sits. Pinholes spread. Once the metal is going, it keeps going.

02Repair or replace

The honest answer, even when it's a smaller job.

A good repair costs less than a new job. A repair on spouting that is finished is money down the drain. The skill is knowing which one you have, and Dylan will tell you straight.

A repair makes sense when

  • One or two joins leak and the rest of the run is sound.
  • A bracket or two has failed and the fascia behind it is solid.
  • An outlet or downpipe is blocked.
  • A single length, joiner or stop-end of uPVC has cracked.

A re-spout is better value when

  • Rust has gone through in several places, or the steel is thin and flaking.
  • More than a few joins leak, or fixing one reveals the next.
  • Whole runs sag or have the wrong fall.
  • The fascia behind is soft or rotten.

Patching rust rarely holds. Sealant and patches cover the hole while the metal around it keeps rusting, and the repair often lets go again. We would rather say so than sell you a repair that doesn't last.

If replacement is the right call, you'll get an itemised quote for it. If a repair will do, that is what we'll quote instead. See spouting replacement for what a full job involves.

Can you just fix the one leaking join?

Yes, where the rest of the spouting is sound. A single join can be cleaned out, resealed or refixed. If we find other joins ready to go, we'll tell you so you aren't paying for a repair visit every winter.

Does sealant or a patch fix rust holes?

Rarely for long. Sealant and patches cover a hole while the metal around it keeps rusting, so the repair tends to fail or another hole opens nearby. A few small spots on otherwise good steel can sometimes be worth treating. Widespread rust means it is time for new spouting.

How do I know whether to repair or replace?

As a rough guide, repair one or two faults on otherwise sound spouting, and replace when rust, leaking joins or sagging are spread along the runs, or the fascia behind is soft. Call Dylan or send a few photos and you'll get an honest opinion. If a repair is enough, that is what we'll quote.

Why does my spouting overflow when it rains?

Usually a blocked outlet or downpipe, a sagging run that holds water, or the wrong fall. On a big roof, rain arriving faster than the downpipes can take it is another cause. A look along the whole run finds which one it is. Regular gutter cleaning prevents the most common cause.

Can you fix storm damage, and will you help with my insurer?

We repair storm damage to spouting, fascia and downpipes. Call and we'll tell you when we can get there. We can provide a written report and quote for your insurer. Check with them first about what your policy covers and what they need to see.

Can cracked uPVC spouting be repaired?

Often a cracked length, joiner or stop-end can be replaced without redoing the whole run, provided matching parts are available and the rest of the spouting is in good shape. If the uPVC has gone brittle along its length, a re-spout is the better option. Marley uPVC spouting carries a 15-year manufacturer guarantee.
