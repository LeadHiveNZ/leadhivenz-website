01Builders & homeowners

Easy to work with on site

On a new build or re-roof, the spouting is one of the last things to go on the roof and one of the first things a homeowner notices. Getting it right comes down to timing, clear communication and finishing details that match the rest of the job.

Dylan works directly with builders, roofers and owner-builders. You get one point of contact who keeps you posted on timing, flags anything on site that will hold things up, and leaves the area clean for the next trade.

02Timing

Fascia before the roof. Spouting after.

The order matters. Get it wrong and you're either waiting on trades or undoing finished work.

  1. Step 01

    Early conversation

    Call when the frame is going up, or earlier. We confirm colours, profiles and timing with you and your builder.

  2. Step 02

    Fascia goes on

    Fascia is fixed to the rafter ends before the roofer lays the iron, so the roof edge has a straight, solid line to finish against.

  3. Step 03

    Iron on, then spouting

    Once the roof iron is down, we fit brackets, spouting and outlets, set to fall toward each downpipe.

  4. Step 04

    Downpipes & finish

    Downpipes run to the stormwater outlets or tank connections on site. Then a water test and a tidy-up for the next trade.

04Materials & colour

Longrun spouting, matched to the roof.

Most new builds and re-roofs go with pre-painted steel to match the roof. Continuous spouting is available for long, clean runs.

Continuous (longrun) spouting

Continuous spouting is steel spouting made in long single lengths to suit each run, so joins are kept to the corners and outlets. Fewer joins means fewer places to leak, and a cleaner line along the eaves. It's available through Central Spouting; ask about the profiles and colours on offer when you get your quote.

Matching the roof and joinery

Most homes match spouting, fascia and downpipes to the roof colour, or to the window joinery for contrast. COLORSTEEL® and ColorCote® name their colours slightly differently, so we confirm the exact match against your roof before ordering. Preview colours with the colour picker on our home page.

05Rural & larger roofs

Sheds, farm buildings and big roofs.

Large roofs shed a lot of water in a short time. On lifestyle blocks and farms across Rangitīkei, Manawatū and South Taranaki, spouting and downpipes need sizing to suit.

  • Bigger profilesA large roof catches more water than a standard house profile can carry. We'll recommend a profile with the capacity to match.
  • More downpipesExtra downpipes shorten the distance water has to travel, and are often the simplest way to stop overflow on long runs.
  • Tank connectionsDownpipes run to your water tanks with the right fittings. Ask about leaf guard or diverters if the tank supplies the house.
  • Built for exposureOpen, windy sites need brackets at the right spacing and products suited to the exposure, especially near the coast.

When should I book you on a new build?

As early as you can, ideally when the frame is going up. Fascia goes on before the roof iron, so we need to be lined up with your roofer. Booking early also leaves time to order the right colours.

Can you price from plans?

Yes. Plans or a roof layout are usually enough for an itemised price on fascia, spouting and downpipes. We confirm the measurements on site before ordering.

Do you supply continuous (longrun) spouting?

Continuous spouting is available. It's made in long lengths to suit each run, so there are far fewer joins. Ask about profiles and colours when you request your quote.

Do I need new spouting if I'm re-roofing?

Not always, but it's the best time to do it. If the existing spouting is old, rusting or the wrong colour for the new roof, replacing it now saves disturbing the new roof edge later. Dylan will tell you honestly if yours has life left in it.

Can you work with my builder or roofer?

Yes. We work alongside builders, roofers and owner-builders and fit around the build programme. One contact handles the fascia, spouting and downpipes from quote to finish.
