01Signs it's time

When patching stops paying off

Spouting rarely fails all at once. It starts with one dripping join, then a rust spot, then a run that holds water. A repair is worth doing while the rest is sound. Once problems show up right around the house, you're paying to patch something that's already on its way out.

On older homes the original steel or early plastic spouting has often simply reached the end of its life. These are the signs to look for. If you're seeing two or three of them, it's worth getting a repair and a replacement priced side by side.

  • Leaks at the joinsDrips from joiners and corners every time it rains. Sealant only buys time once the spouting around it has moved or corroded.
  • Rust and flaking paintBrown streaks, bubbling paint or pinholes in steel spouting. Rust works from the inside, so what you can see is the least of it.
  • Sagging runsSpouting that dips between brackets holds water instead of moving it. Standing water speeds up corrosion and adds weight the brackets weren't built for.
  • Overflow in heavy rainWater spilling over the front edge even when the spouting is clean. It may be undersized for the roof, short on downpipes or set with the wrong fall.
  • Staining on walls and fasciaDark streaks down the weatherboards or soffit, or paint peeling along the fascia. Water is going where it shouldn't, and the timber behind may be suffering.
  • A building report flags itSelling or buying? Pre-purchase building reports often note spouting and fascia condition. A written, itemised quote helps either side of the deal.

02The job, step by step

What a re-spout actually involves.

No surprises on the day. This is what happens, in order, on a typical re-spout.

Most single-storey homes are done in a day. Two-storey homes and jobs with fascia work take longer, and your quote says how long to expect.

  1. Step 01

    Strip the old spouting

    Old spouting, brackets and tired downpipes come off carefully, without damaging the roof iron or paintwork. We take all of it away.

  2. Step 02

    Check the fascia

    With the spouting off, the fascia is exposed for the first time in years. If there's rot, we show you and agree the cost before doing anything extra.

  3. Step 03

    Brackets and falls

    New brackets go up at the right spacing, set to a steady fall toward each outlet, so water moves along the spouting instead of sitting in it.

  4. Step 04

    New spouting

    Spouting is fitted with joins, corners, stop ends and outlets sealed and fixed properly. Each run is checked for line and level.

  5. Step 05

    Downpipes

    Downpipes are replaced, or added where the roof needs more, and connected to your existing stormwater outlets or tank.

  6. Step 06

    Test with water

    We run water through the system and watch it drain, checking every join and outlet before calling it done.

  7. Step 07

    Clean-up and walk-round

    Offcuts, screws and old spouting cleared from the gutters, lawn and paths. Then we walk round the house with you.

03Materials

Steel, uPVC or longrun. The honest version.

All three are good products, and each suits some homes better than others. The right one depends on your roof, your budget and how close you live to the sea.

Pre-painted steel

COLORSTEEL® & ColorCote®

Strong, rigid and crisp, in colours made to match New Zealand roofs. Sectional steel spouting comes in set lengths joined on site.

Good for: matching a steel roof, character homes, and a sharp, straight line along the eaves.

Watch for: steel can corrode where it's scratched, where wet debris sits, or under run-off from copper or lead flashings. Near the sea, sheltered sections need regular washing.

ColorCote and COLORSTEEL steel rainwater goods carry manufacturer warranties that depend on product and exposure zone; we'll tell you exactly what applies to your home.

uPVC

Marley spouting

Light, rust-free and quick to fit. Stormcloud (quarter round) and Typhoon (half round) are the common choices.

Good for: cost-conscious re-spouts, sheds and garages, and spots where rust is the main worry.

Watch for: fewer colours than steel. uPVC moves with temperature, so expansion joints and bracket spacing have to be right.

Marley uPVC spouting carries a 15-year manufacturer guarantee.

Continuous / longrun

Continuous spouting

Steel spouting made in long single lengths to suit each run, so joins are kept to the corners and outlets.

Good for: long, straight eaves, rural homes and sheds, and anyone tired of leaking joins.

Watch for: it's made to length for your house, so profile and colour choices depend on what's available at the time.

Available on request. Ask about profiles and colours when you get your quote.

Close to the coast? Homes near the sea, in places like Castlecliff, Foxton Beach and Pātea, sit in a harsher exposure zone. That changes which products suit and what the warranty covers, so we check your exposure before recommending anything.

04Profiles & colour

Choose the shape, then the colour.

The profile is the shape of the spouting face. Match it to the age and style of the house, and to how much water your roof sheds.

Quad · Stormcloud

Quarter round

The everyday Kiwi profile. A smooth curved face that suits most homes, old and new.

Typhoon · on brackets

Half round

A traditional round gutter hung on brackets. Right at home on villas, bungalows and character homes.

Square line

Box

Crisp, square lines for modern and architectural homes, and a neat match for flat-faced fascia.

Ogee · Old Gothic

OG

A decorative stepped curve that throws a sharp shadow line along the eaves.

05Pricing

What affects the price.

We don't publish a per-metre rate, because no two rooflines are the same. These are the things that move the number, and each one is listed on your quote.

  • Length of spoutingMeasured around every eave, including bay windows, porches and the garage. It's the biggest single factor.
  • Number of downpipesEach downpipe adds an outlet, offsets and fixings. Some homes need more than they have now to stop overflow.
  • Storeys and accessTwo-storey walls, steep sections and tight boundaries take longer, and may need scaffolding or edge protection.
  • Profile and materialuPVC, sectional steel and continuous spouting are priced differently, and some profiles cost more than others.
  • Removing the old spoutingStripping the old spouting and downpipes and taking them away is part of the job, and it's shown on the quote.
  • What's behind itIf rotten fascia turns up once the spouting is off, we stop, show you and price it before carrying on. No surprises on the invoice.

How much does it cost to replace spouting?

It depends on the length of spouting, the number of downpipes, the material and profile, access, and whether there's fascia work once the old spouting is off. We measure up and give you a free, itemised written quote, so you can see where every dollar goes and compare it properly.

How long does a re-spout take?

Most single-storey homes are done in a day. Two-storey homes, long rural roofs and jobs that include fascia take longer. Your quote tells you what to expect, and we keep you posted if the weather turns.

Should I replace the downpipes at the same time?

Usually, yes. Old downpipes are often the same age as the spouting and fail soon after it. Doing them together means everything matches and the access is only set up once. If yours are in good condition, we'll say so and leave them.

Can you match my roof colour?

In most cases. COLORSTEEL® and ColorCote® steel come in colours made to match New Zealand roofs, and Marley uPVC comes in a smaller range. Try the colour picker on our home page, then ask Dylan for a physical sample before you decide.

What happens if the fascia is rotten?

We find out once the old spouting comes off. Dylan shows you what he's found and agrees the cost with you before doing any extra work. Short sections can be repaired, and longer runs replaced in timber or pre-painted steel. More on fascia repair and replacement.

Do I need to be home while you work?

Not usually. As long as we have safe access to the outside of the house, we can measure up and install while you're at work. We'll walk round with you when it's finished, or catch up with you later if you're out.
