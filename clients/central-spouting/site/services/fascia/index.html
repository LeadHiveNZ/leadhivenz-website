Fascia · Whanganui & beyond

Rotten fascia, fixed for good.

Fascia is the board along the edge of your roof that the spouting hangs on. When it rots, the spouting goes with it. Dylan preps and paints, repairs, replaces or upgrades fascia, and converts leaking internal gutters on older homes to external spouting.

01Why fascia fails

Timber fascia has a hard life

Fascia sits right at the edge of the roof, facing the weather, with water running past it every time it rains. On older villas and bungalows it's often the original timber, and decades of sun, rain and repainting take their toll. It's common in Whanganui suburbs like Gonville, St Johns Hill and Durie Hill, and on older homes across Palmerston North and Rangitīkei.

The usual culprits

  • Weather. Sun dries and splits the timber, then rain and wind drive water into the cracks.
  • Paint failure. Once paint lifts along the top edge, water soaks in where you can't see it. It's often the first sign of trouble.
  • Leaking spouting. Water from a failed join or an overflowing run tracks back behind the spouting and sits against the fascia. Years of that and the timber goes soft.
  • Borer. Older and untreated timber can carry borer. Fine holes and powdery dust mean the board is weaker than it looks.

How to check yours

From the ground, look for peeling paint, dark staining or a wavy line along the roof edge. If you can reach it safely, a screwdriver that sinks into the timber tells you all you need to know. Or send Dylan a few photos and let him take a look.

02Your options

Paint, repair, replace or go steel.

The right fix depends on the state of the timber, how far any rot has spread and how long you want it to last. Dylan shows you what he finds and talks you through each option.

Option 01

Prep and paint

Where the timber is still sound and it's only the paint that's failing, we sand back the flaking paint, prime any bare wood and repaint, so the board is protected again.

Best when: the board is solid and straight, and you like the look of painted timber.

Option 02

Repair sections

Where rot is limited to a few spots, we cut out the damaged lengths and splice in new timber, ready for painting.

Best when: the rest of the board is sound and you're happy to keep painting it.

Option 03

Replace with steel fascia

We remove the timber and fit pre-painted steel fascia in a colour to match the spouting or roof. Nothing to rot, and no more scraping and repainting.

Best when: the timber is past saving, or you're re-spouting and want the roofline sorted for good.

Option 04

Cover the existing fascia

Where suitable, a pre-painted steel cover can go over sound timber for a low-maintenance finish without stripping everything off.

Best when: the board is solid and straight. We won't cover rot: it keeps spreading behind the steel where you can't see it.

Steel fascia warranties work like spouting: they depend on the product and your exposure zone, and homes close to the sea are treated differently. We'll tell you exactly what applies to your home.

03Internal gutters

Villa with internal gutters? Consider going external.

Many older Whanganui homes have internal (box) gutters hidden behind the fascia or a parapet. When they leak, the water goes inside: into the ceiling, the walls and the framing.

Converting to external spouting moves the water to the outside of the house, where a leak is a drip on the path instead of a stain on the ceiling. The usual approach is to remove the old internal gutter, build out the roof edge so the iron finishes over new fascia, then hang external spouting with new downpipes. Where the roof iron needs extending or replacing, we work with a roofer.

Not every roof suits it. Where the iron can't be extended, the eaves are shallow, or the house has heritage detail worth keeping, conversion may not be the right call, and Dylan will tell you. Sometimes a repair to the existing gutter is the better option.

Signs of trouble

Is your internal gutter failing?

  • Water stains on ceilings near the outside walls
  • Paint bubbling or peeling high on interior walls
  • A damp smell or mould in one corner of a room
  • Water spilling over the front of the fascia in heavy rain
  • Rust or old patch repairs visible in the gutter

04What's involved

How a fascia job runs.

Most fascia work happens alongside a re-spout, because the spouting has to come off anyway. Nothing extra is done without your say-so.

  1. Step 01

    Look & quote

    Dylan inspects the fascia, probes for soft spots and checks the roof edge. You get an itemised quote with the options side by side.

  2. Step 02

    Strip & check

    The spouting comes off, then the damaged fascia. If the rafter ends behind need attention, we tell you before going any further.

  3. Step 03

    New fascia

    New timber or pre-painted steel fascia is fixed straight and true, giving the spouting a solid line to hang from.

  4. Step 04

    Spouting & clean-up

    Spouting goes back up, or new spouting goes on, then a water test, a tidy-up and a walk-round with you.

05Fascia + spouting

Do both at once.

If your fascia needs work, your spouting is probably due too. The reverse is often true as well.

The spouting has to come off to replace fascia properly. Putting old spouting back on new fascia means paying for removal twice when that spouting fails a few years later. Doing both together gives you one job, one colour scheme and a roofline that's sorted.

Going the other way, if you're re-spouting and the fascia is tired but sound, it's a good moment to fit a steel cover or repaint while there's clear access.

How do I know if my fascia is rotten?

Peeling paint along the top edge, dark staining, a wavy roof edge or spouting pulling away are the usual signs. If the timber gives under a screwdriver, it's soft. Send Dylan a few photos and he'll tell you what he thinks.

Is steel fascia better than timber?

It's lower maintenance. Pre-painted steel doesn't rot, split or need repainting, and it comes in colours to match your spouting and roof. Timber still suits some character homes, and it's often the right choice for a short repair. Dylan will talk you through both.

Can you cover my old fascia instead of replacing it?

Where the timber is sound and straight, yes, a pre-painted steel cover can be a good option. We won't cover rotten timber, because the rot keeps spreading behind the cover where you can't see it.

Can you convert internal gutters to external spouting?

Often, yes. Moving the water to the outside of the house means a leak can no longer reach your ceilings or walls. Not every roof suits it, though, and Dylan will tell you honestly if yours doesn't.

Do I need new spouting when the fascia is replaced?

Not always, but the spouting has to come off to replace the fascia properly. If your spouting is old, replacing both together usually makes sense. If it's in good condition, we can put it back up.
