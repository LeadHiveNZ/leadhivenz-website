Fences built for a Christchurch nor'wester

When a nor'west gale comes through, the fences that go over are the ones with rotten posts or rails that were never fixed properly. Jamie sets posts in concrete, uses treated timber rated for ground contact, and fixes palings and rails so the whole fence works together.

Not every fence needs replacing. Often the palings are fine and only a couple of posts or rails have gone. Jamie will tell you what can be saved, and if the timber is sound but grey and tired, a clean and a coat of stain can transform it, like the fence restoration shown below.

Boundary fences are usually a shared cost with your neighbour under the Fencing Act. A clear written quote makes that conversation a lot easier, and Jamie is happy to talk it through with both of you.

FAQ · Fences & gates

Common questions

Something else on your mind? Call Jamie on 021 704 252.

Do I need consent for a new fence?

Most residential fences do not need building consent, but the Christchurch District Plan has height rules, particularly along the road boundary. Jamie will check what applies before building.

Who pays for a boundary fence?

Under the Fencing Act, neighbours generally share the cost of an adequate boundary fence. A written quote helps you agree on the work and the split before it starts.

Can you just repair part of my fence?

Yes. Replacing a few posts, rails or palings is often all that is needed. Jamie will give you an honest view on whether a repair will last.

My gate sags and will not latch. Can that be fixed?

Usually, yes. Re-hanging with stronger hinges, adding a brace and resetting the latch fixes most sagging gates. If the frame has rotted, a new gate is built to fit.
