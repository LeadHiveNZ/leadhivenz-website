Good to know

Deck and fence questions, answered.

Do I need a building consent for a deck?

Not if it's not possible to fall more than 1.5 m from the deck, even if it collapses (Building Act Schedule 1). The test is the fall height to the ground below, not how high the deck looks, so a deck on a sloping Wellington section often needs consent even when it sits close to the house. We check this at the site visit.

When does a deck need a balustrade?

Where someone could fall 1 m or more, the Building Code (clause F4) requires a barrier. That applies whether or not the deck needs a consent. We include the balustrade in the design and the quote.

Do I need consent for a fence?

Fences up to 2.5 m high are exempt from building consent, but pool fences are not. Your district plan may also cap boundary fences at around 2 m, so check before you go tall. We'll tell you what applies to your address.

Does my neighbour have to pay for half the boundary fence?

Under the Fencing Act 1978, neighbours generally share the cost of an adequate boundary fence. Talk to your neighbour first. If you can't agree, the Act sets out a formal notice process. We can quote the fence so you both have a number to work from.

How much does a deck cost per square metre?

As an indicative Wellington guide: ground-level pine decks $350–550 per m², kwila or composite $500–900, hillside decks with a balustrade $700–1,200, and engineered steep-site decks $900–1,500 or more. GST inclusive, not a quote. Fences are priced per metre on site.
