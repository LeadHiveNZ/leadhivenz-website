Most sections here have a bank somewhere.

Behind the house, beside the driveway, or under the lawn you'd like to be flat. A good wall gives you usable ground back. A bad one leans, bulges and ends up costing you twice.

From Tawa to Titahi Bay, the ground is mostly clay on a slope, and clay holds water. Water is what pushes walls over. So every wall we build starts with what goes behind it, not the timber you see out front. Because we own the diggers and trucks, the dig-out, cartage and backfill are done by the same crew that builds the wall.

What goes behind the wall

Drainage is what keeps a wall standing.

Water pressure behind a wall is the main reason walls fail. This is what we put behind every one, whatever it's made of.

01Drainage coil at the basePerforated drain pipe in a filter sock, laid along the footing with a fall towards the outlet.
02Scoria backfillFree-draining scoria behind the wall, so water drops to the coil instead of building pressure.
03Filter clothGeotextile between the scoria and your soil, so clay can't silt the drainage up over time.
04An approved outletThe drain runs to stormwater or another approved discharge point. A drain that goes nowhere doesn't count.
Consents

Do you need consent for your wall?

Short answer: it depends on height and what's above it. We tell you which camp you're in at the site visit, before you spend anything.

  • Up to 1.5 m, with no surcharge: exempt. A wall retaining 1.5 m of ground or less, with nothing loading it from above, doesn't need building consent. It still has to meet the Building Code, drainage included.
  • Over 1.5 m, or surcharged: consent and an engineer. A surcharge is a driveway, building, pool, another wall or sloping ground above the wall. Any of those, or more than 1.5 m retained, and you need building consent and normally an engineer's design. We build to that design.
  • Wellington City: resource consent may apply too. Wellington City Council says a wall near a boundary or over 2.5 m high may need resource consent, on top of building consent.

Good to know

Retaining wall questions, answered.

Do I need a building consent for a retaining wall?

Not if the wall retains 1.5 m of ground or less and has no surcharge on it. That's exempt under Schedule 1 of the Building Act, but it still has to meet the Building Code, drainage included. A wall retaining more than 1.5 m, or any wall with a surcharge, needs building consent and normally an engineer. We tell you which camp you're in at the site visit.

What counts as a surcharge?

Anything adding load to the ground behind the wall: a driveway or parking area, a building, a pool, another retaining wall, or the ground sloping up steeply above it. On Wellington hillsides that's common, which is why plenty of walls under 1.5 m still need consent.

Could I need resource consent as well?

Possibly. Wellington City Council says a retaining wall may need resource consent if it's near a boundary or over 2.5 m high. That's separate from building consent. Porirua, Hutt and Kāpiti councils have their own district plan rules, so we check for your address.

Why does drainage behind a wall matter so much?

Water pressure building up behind a wall is the main reason walls lean, bulge and fail. We lay a drainage coil at the base, backfill with free-draining scoria, wrap it in filter cloth so the soil can't clog it, and run the drain to an approved outlet.

Timber, block or crib: which should I choose?

Timber (H5 posts, H4 rails) is the cheapest and suits most garden walls and banks. Block or concrete costs more but lasts longer and looks tidier, especially on a driveway or boundary. Crib walls suit taller or engineered situations. We'll give you options with prices at the site visit.

How much does a retaining wall cost in Wellington?

As an indicative guide, a timber wall runs about $200–400 per m² of wall face, plus $50–150 per m² for drainage and waterproofing. A short timber garden wall (up to about 6 m long and 1 m high) is typically $3,240–8,100 all-in. Engineered or consented walls are usually $770–1,650 per m². Those are GST-inclusive ranges, not a quote.
