Wet hire, explained

You get the machine and the person who runs it.

Wet hire means the digger or truck comes with an operator. You don't need a licence, a trailer or a weekend of practice. You tell us what you want dug, moved or carted, and we do it.

On a tight Wellington section that matters. An operator who knows the machine can work around fences, lawns, retaining and the neighbour's driveway without leaving damage behind. Because we also build driveways, walls and slabs, we dig to the levels the next trade actually needs.

Cleanfill vs demolition waste

What goes in the truck changes what it costs to tip.

Keep loads separate and you pay less, because mixed loads cost more to tip. Tip fees are passed through at cost.

CleanCleanfillClean natural material only: soil, clay and rock. No concrete, rubble, timber, rubbish or green waste. Wellington's Southern Landfill charges $95.50 a tonne for domestic cleanfill.
MixedDemolition & mixed wasteOld concrete, asphalt, timber, rubble and anything mixed with them. It goes to the right facility for that waste and costs more to tip than cleanfill.
Before we dig

beforeUdig is a free service that shows where power, gas, water, telecommunications and fibre run under or near your property. We request it before any digging, then confirm on site.

  • Underground services located from the beforeUdig plans, and any private pipes or cables you know about.

Good to know

Digger and truck hire questions.

What does “wet hire” mean?

Wet hire means the machine comes with an operator. You don't drive anything: you book the digger or truck and the person who runs it, and we do the work. Expect an hourly rate with a minimum charge, plus travel or cartage and any tip fees.

How much does a digger and operator cost?

As an indicative Wellington guide, $100–160 an hour depending on the size of machine, GST inclusive. A minimum charge applies, and cartage and tip fees are extra. Half-day and full-day rates work out better for bigger jobs. Ask Adam for current rates for your job.

Can you take away the soil and rubble?

Yes, in our own tip trucks. Clean natural fill (soil, clay and rock) can go as cleanfill. Anything with concrete, rubble, timber or rubbish in it is demolition or mixed waste, which goes elsewhere and costs more to tip. Keeping loads separate keeps your tip fees down, and we pass tip fees through at cost.

Do underground services need to be checked before digging?

Yes. Before any dig we make a free beforeUdig request, which shows where power, gas, water, telecommunications and fibre run under or near your property. If you know of private pipes or cables on your section, tell us at the site visit too.

Is there a minimum hire?

Yes. Every booking has a minimum charge to cover getting the machine to site and back. For anything more than a quick job, a half-day or full-day booking is better value. We'll suggest the right booking when you tell us what you need done.
