Who we are

A warm place to belong, right here in our community.

Aroha Seniors Charitable Trust is a registered charity based in Flat Bush, Auckland. We create welcoming spaces where older people, vulnerable adults and adults living with disability can make friends, stay active and connected, and get the support they need.

Everything we do is guided by one word: aroha.

Read our story
aroha
a·ro·ha  ·  te reo Māori

Love, compassion, empathy and care for one another.

It's our name, and it's how we look after every person who walks through our door.
1 in 5frail older New Zealanders living at home report extreme loneliness.

Loneliness isn't just sad. It can affect health, confidence and independence.

A friendly face, a shared meal or a day out can change that. That's why we're here, and why every gathering, phone call and outing matters.

Get in touch
Who can come along?

Our programmes are for older people (seniors and kaumātua), vulnerable adults and adults living with disability in Flat Bush and the surrounding area. Whānau and carers are welcome too.

Does it cost anything?

Get in touch and we'll talk you through what's on and any costs before you come along, so there are no surprises.

I don't drive. Can I still take part?

Please still get in touch. Let us know when you call and we'll talk through the best way to get you there.

English isn't my first language. Is that okay?

Of course. Our community is wonderfully diverse. Tell us which language you're most comfortable in, and we'll do our best to help.

Can I get in touch for a parent or grandparent?

Absolutely. Call, text or send us a message and we'll take it from there, with their permission of course.

How can I help?

You can volunteer, make a donation or partner with us. Every bit of help means more older people feel connected and cared for.

About us

Built on aroha.

We're a community charity with a simple belief: our elders deserve to feel connected, valued and cared for, every single day.

Our story

From a community group to a charitable trust.

Aroha Seniors began as a community group, started by people who could see how many older people around them were spending long days on their own.

The idea was simple: bring people together. Share kai and kōrero. Get out and about. Keep in touch with those who can't easily leave home, and help people find their way through the services and systems that can make later life feel complicated.

In 2025 we were registered as a charity, and in 2026 we became the Aroha Seniors Charitable Trust. It's a step that lets us work with funders and partners, grow our programmes and reach more people who need us.

Home/What we do

What we do

Ways we bring people together.

From gatherings and outings to a friendly phone call or help with a new tablet, here's how we keep older people in our community connected.

01

Friendship gatherings

Regular get-togethers where the kettle's always on. Share kai and kōrero, play games, enjoy some music and make new friends.

  • A warm welcome, whether it's your first time or your fiftieth
  • Shared kai, cups of tea and plenty of conversation
  • Games, music, crafts and celebrations through the year
Good for: anyone who'd like more company
02

Outings & group trips

Getting out and about can be hard when you don't drive or don't want to go alone. Our group outings mean everyone can enjoy a day out, together.

  • Day trips to places of interest around Auckland
  • Planned with older people in mind: pace, seating and accessibility
  • A lovely way to meet new people
Good for: anyone who misses getting out
03

Companionship & check-ins

For people who find it hard to leave home, a regular friendly call or visit can make all the difference to their week.

  • Friendly phone calls to check in and have a chat
  • Visits for people who are housebound or recovering
  • A link back to the wider group when you're ready
Good for: people living alone or at home most of the time
04

Digital confidence

Phones, tablets and the internet can keep you close to whānau near and far, once you know how. We help patiently, at your pace.

  • Video calls with family here and overseas
  • Messaging, photos and Facebook
  • Staying safe from scams online
Good for: beginners and anyone who's ever felt stuck
05

Advice, information & advocacy

Getting older can come with a lot of forms, phone calls and systems. We help people understand their options and connect with the right services, and we speak up for our elders.

  • Help finding the right health, community and government services
  • Support with forms, letters and appointments
  • A voice for older people in our community
Good for: anyone unsure where to turn
06

Disability & wellbeing support

We're here for vulnerable adults and adults living with disability too, and for the whānau and carers who support them.

  • Inclusive activities everyone can enjoy
  • Connection and support for carers
  • Help connecting with specialist services
Good for: adults living with disability and their whānau
