# SR Shutters & Screens: Design & Conversion Brief

Client: SR Shutters & Screens (Shane), Brisbane QLD
Prepared by: LeadHive research, 26 Oct 2026
Brand: black circle logo, gold script "SR" monogram, gold serif caps "SHUTTERS & SCREENS"
Goal: a site that makes SR look like the best shutters-and-screens operator in the country, converts homeowners into free-measure bookings, and makes Google reviews a habit.

Items marked **[CONFIRM]** are facts only Shane can supply (licence number, years trading, warranty terms, finance provider, real rating). Do not publish them until he confirms them.

---

## 0. Executive summary (read this first)

1. **The national brands don't install. Shane does.** Crimsafe ("190 authorised suppliers"), Norman, Luxaflex, Hunter Douglas and Invisi-Gard all route you to "a dealer in your area". SR's best position is the opposite: **one owner, one standard, every job**. "Measured, made right and fitted by Shane."
2. **Brisbane competitors look tired and cluttered.** Shutterup has duplicate nav, keyword-stuffed copy, typos, a "blinds-brisbane-test" URL and a 4.9 rating with no review count. Davcon has a broken cookie banner with `{title}` placeholders, five different CTA labels and an internal "Meet Our Team-2025 Edits" menu item. FKR shows "© 2023" and says "24/7" while its footer lists Mon to Fri hours. Diamond Blinds has blank SVG placeholder images and no warranty term. The bar is low, and a clean, editorial, black-and-gold site clears it easily.
3. **Nobody in the set does before/after properly.** Not one of the 12 big players or 4 locals has a before/after on its home page. That is SR's signature section.
4. **Discount-led heroes cheapen the premium brands.** Luxaflex, Hunter Douglas, Wynstan, Accent and Davcon all lead with a sale. SR should lead with craft and proof, and keep any offer in a quiet secondary slot.
5. **Reviews are the moat.** The locals winning on reviews (Davcon 4.9 from 270 Google reviews, Diamond 5.0 from 48+) display them as plain text with no dates. SR will show Google-style cards with first name, suburb, product, date and a link-out, fed by a post-install review system.

### 2d. Australian trust markers (and how to word them truthfully)

- **QBCC licence number** in the header strip or footer, linked to the QBCC licence search. The QBCC requires a licence for building work valued over $3,300. Commentary on the Act excludes internal blinds, curtains and internal window shutters, but external shutters, screens and doors over the threshold generally need one. **[CONFIRM licence number and class]**. Diamond and Davcon show theirs; Shutterup and FKR don't.
- **ABN** in the footer (Prowler Proof and Wynstan both show theirs).
- **Australian-made**: only claim this if SR's products are made in Australia. Name the component or partner (e.g. "Australian-made aluminium shutters", "mesh made in QLD"). The Australian Made logo is licensed, so don't use it unless the supplier holds it.
- **Australian Standards (security screens):** name the standard, because "all Australian Standards" (Davcon) is weak. The current standard is **AS 5039.1:2023** (performance, covering dynamic impact, jemmy, pull, probe and knife shear) with **AS 5039.3:2023** (test methods). **AS 5041-2003** is the older test-method standard that many brands still cite. Suggested wording: "Security screens tested to AS 5039 (dynamic impact, knife shear, jemmy and pull tests)". Only use it once Shane confirms his product carries the certification.
- **Wind / storm:** Brisbane is broadly non-cyclonic (wind region A/B depending on site, under AS/NZS 1170.2). Don't sell "cyclone-rated" for Brisbane homes. Say "built for Brisbane storm season", "wind-rated slats for exposed sites" and "hail and debris protection" (roller shutters). Mention cyclone-rated products only if Shane supplies them (e.g. Invisi-Maxx style).
- **Free measure and quote, obligation-free.** It is universal in this category, so make it specific: "Free in-home measure & quote, itemised in writing within 24 hours [CONFIRM]".
- **Child safety:** corded blinds must meet ACCC mandatory standards. Add a short line and a footer link (Accent and Shutterup link to child-safety pages).
- **Insurance:** "Fully insured, $X public liability [CONFIRM]".
- **Finance:** Humm (Prowler, Davcon, Invisi-Gard), Zip and Humm (Wynstan). Show only if Shane signs up. Use the provider's required disclosure ("Approved applicants only. Fees, terms and conditions apply.").

### 4b. Typography (Google Fonts)

The logo's gold serif caps sit closest to Trajan / Cinzel. Echo them in small-caps eyebrows only, and never set body copy in a display serif.

**Pairing A (recommended): Cormorant Garamond + Manrope**

- Body/UI: Manrope 400/500/600 (body 17 to 18px, buttons 15px/600, uppercase +0.12em tracking for buttons and eyebrows).

**Pairing B (alternative, more contemporary): Playfair Display + Jost**
- Playfair Display 500 for headings (higher contrast, editorial), Jost 400/500 for body (geometric, clean).

Type scale (desktop / mobile): H1 72 / 44, H2 52 / 34, H3 28 / 22, body 18 / 17, small 14, eyebrow 13. Line-height: 1.05 for display, 1.6 for body. Measure: 60 to 70 characters.

Rules: no script fonts beyond the logo monogram. No all-caps headings in the serif (caps are for the Cinzel eyebrows and buttons only). Use the serif for numerals in trust stats.

### 4c. Spacing, radius, shadow, lines

- 8px base scale: 4, 8, 12, 16, 24, 32, 48, 64, 96, 128, 160.
- Section padding: 128 to 160px desktop, 72 to 88px mobile. Container max 1240px, plus a 1440px full-bleed for imagery. Side gutters: 24px mobile, 48px tablet, 64px desktop.
- **Radius: 2px** on buttons, inputs and cards, and 0 on images. Square corners read architectural and premium, and echo shutter frames. Pills are reserved for small chips only.
- **Shadows:** none on dark sections, where depth comes from tone (onyx, charcoal, graphite). On ivory, use one soft shadow only: `0 24px 48px -28px rgba(11,11,12,.35)`.
- **Gold hairlines:** 1px `rgba(198,161,91,.4)`. Used as section dividers, under eyebrows (a 48px line), between trust items, and as the process timeline.
- **Signature motif, "louvre lines":** three stacked 1px gold hairlines 6px apart, about 64px wide, abstracting shutter blades. Use it beside eyebrows, in the footer, and as the favicon backdrop. It is the brand's own device and costs nothing.

### 4d. Image treatment

- Large editorial photography: real SR jobs, shot in the morning or late afternoon so light rakes through the louvres. Wide crops, minimal props, no people facing camera except Shane.
- Hero overlay: `linear-gradient(180deg, rgba(11,11,12,.20) 0%, rgba(11,11,12,.45) 55%, rgba(11,11,12,.85) 100%)`, plus a left-side radial for headline legibility.
- Grade: warm and slightly desaturated (lift blacks a touch, warm highlights). Keep whites in shutters clean, never yellow.
- Security screens: dusk or night shots with warm interior light glowing behind the mesh (the Prowler Proof idea, done better).
- Product cards: 4:5 portrait. Gallery: a mix of 4:5, 1:1 and 16:9 in an asymmetric grid. Before/after: identical 3:2 framing.
- Photo brief for Shane: tripod, same position for before/after, lights on and blinds open, shoot at the end of every job (10 shots: wide, mid, louvre detail, hinge/frame detail, outside looking in). This one habit feeds the gallery, suburb pages, review emails and social.
- Demo placeholders: if AI or stock images are used in the demo, label them internally and replace before launch. Never pass stock off as SR's work.
- Format: AVIF/WebP, `srcset`, lazy-load below the fold, hero preloaded, LCP under 2.5s on 4G.

### 4e. Motion rules

- Reveal on scroll: fade-up 16px, 700ms, `cubic-bezier(.2,.7,.2,1)`, 80ms stagger between siblings, once only (IntersectionObserver, 15% threshold).
- One signature moment: on first load the hero image is revealed by horizontal "louvre" slats opening (CSS mask, under 1.1s total), then never repeated. Everything else stays quiet.
- Hover: images scale to 1.03 over 600ms; gold underline draws left to right over 300ms; buttons shift 1px with a light glow.
- Before/after: draggable handle with a gold knob, keyboard accessible (arrow keys), with no auto-animation beyond one gentle 15% nudge the first time it enters view.
- No carousels that auto-rotate (Crimsafe, Invisi-Gard, Wynstan and Completehome all use sliders, and they hide content). No parallax on mobile, no cursor followers, no counters spinning up from zero, no particle effects.
- `prefers-reduced-motion`: disable all transforms and keep only opacity fades.

### 4f. Mobile-first

- Sticky bottom bar: "Call Shane" | "Free Quote" (gold), 56px tall plus safe-area inset, shown after the hero, hidden over the form.
- The hero CTA must be visible without scrolling on a 375x667 screen. Keep the headline to two to three lines at 44px.
- Tap targets are at least 48px. The phone is a `tel:` link everywhere. The form uses `inputmode="tel"`, and suburb autocomplete is limited to SEQ.

### 5f. FAQs (Brisbane homeowners)

**1. Will plantation shutters actually keep my house cooler in a Brisbane summer?**
Yes. Closed shutters block direct sun and create an insulating air gap between the glass and the room, so west-facing rooms heat up far less in the afternoon. Tilt the blades up to bounce light off the ceiling while keeping the heat and glare out. For the hottest western windows, Shane may suggest pairing shutters with a solar-reflective security mesh outside.

**2. PVC or basswood: which is better for Queensland?**
For bathrooms, laundries, kitchens and anywhere near moisture, PVC every time. It won't swell, warp or peel in Brisbane humidity, and you can wipe it clean. Basswood is a real timber that is lighter and suits wide living-area windows and stained finishes, but it should be kept away from wet areas. Many homes end up with both: PVC in the wet rooms, basswood in the living areas. Aluminium is the choice for outdoors.

**3. Are your shutters and screens built for storm season?**
Our aluminium outdoor shutters and roller shutters are built to handle Brisbane's summer storms, with options for wind-rated slats on exposed sites. Roller shutters add protection against hail and flying debris, and motorised models can include battery back-up so they still close in a blackout. If you're on an exposed ridge or near the bay, Shane will spec accordingly at the measure.

**4. How secure are your security screens?**
Our stainless steel security screens and doors are tested to the Australian Standard for security screens (AS 5039), which covers impact, knife-shear, jemmy and pull tests, and they are fitted with quality locks. **[CONFIRM product and lock brand]** That means you can leave doors and windows open for the breeze without leaving the house open to intruders. A screen is only as strong as its installation, which is why Shane fits every one himself.

**5. How long does it take from quote to install?**
Most plantation shutter and security screen orders are installed within X to Y weeks of you approving the quote, because everything is custom made to measure. **[CONFIRM]** Fly screens and repairs are usually faster. We give you a firm install date when you order, and we'll tell you straight away if anything changes.

**6. What warranty do I get?**
Two layers. First, the manufacturer's warranty on the product itself (for example, up to X years on shutters and X years on security mesh **[CONFIRM per product]**). Second, our own SR Workmanship Guarantee on the installation **[CONFIRM term]**. If a panel drops, a hinge loosens or a door needs adjusting, Shane comes back and fixes it. We also visit at 12 months for a free adjustment.

- **Display reviews as they were written.** Don't edit them. Show the occasional 4-star; Wynstan's slightly imperfect Trustpilot reviews read as more believable.

### 6b. The `/review` page and QR flow

- **Get the link:** Google Business Profile, then Read reviews, then Get more reviews, then Copy. This gives the short review link (commonly `https://g.page/r/XXXXXXXX/review`). The fallback is `https://search.google.com/local/writereview?placeid=PLACE_ID`. Test it in a private window before printing anything.
- **Own the redirect:** print and send `srshutters.com.au/review` **[domain TBC]**, which 302-redirects to the Google link. If Google changes the link format, nothing printed breaks, and LeadHive can count clicks.
- **Optional page version** (instead of a plain redirect): photo of the finished work, "Thanks for choosing SR, [it means the world to a local business]", one gold button "Leave a Google review (takes 30 seconds)", three prompt ideas ("What did we install? Which suburb? How was the experience?"), and a small, separate "Need something adjusted? Call Shane on 07 XXXX XXXX" line.
- **No review gating.** Don't build the "Were you happy? Yes goes to Google, No goes to a private form" funnel. Google's policy prohibits merchants from "selectively solicit[ing] positive reviews from customers", and the ACCC treats selectively asking for reviews based on how likely they are to be positive as misleading. **Every customer gets the same Google link.** A support line is fine; a filter is not.
- **No incentives** (discounts, prize draws, gift cards) tied to reviews. Google prohibits them, and the ACCC requires disclosure. No staff quotas, and no asking for specific content. Don't pressure people "on premises": a leave-behind card is fine, and standing over them with a phone is not.
- **QR leave-behind:** a black, heavyweight A6 card with a gold foil SR monogram. Front: "Enjoy your new shutters." Back: care tips, a QR code to `/review`, Shane's mobile, and "12-month adjustment visit, on us". Shane leaves it on the bench at handover. A matching QR also goes on the invoice PDF and on the website's leave-a-review band (for desktop visitors).

### 6e. Schema and AggregateRating: rules and recommendation

- **What Google says:** a LocalBusiness or Organization showing reviews **about itself** on its own site is "self-serving" and **ineligible for review star rich results**, including when they come through an embedded Google widget. Google also says "Don't aggregate reviews or ratings from other websites", any AggregateRating must be **visible on the page**, and it needs `ratingValue` plus `ratingCount` or `reviewCount`.
- **Recommendation:**
  1. Ship `HomeAndConstructionBusiness` (a LocalBusiness subtype) JSON-LD on every page: name, logo, image, telephone, address or `areaServed` (list the regions), `openingHoursSpecification`, `priceRange`, `sameAs` (the Google Business Profile URL, Facebook, Instagram), `hasOfferCatalog` (services). Add `Service` schema on service pages and `FAQPage` on the FAQ.
  2. **Do not** put AggregateRating copied from Google reviews into the markup. It won't produce stars, it breaches the "don't aggregate from other websites" guideline, and it risks a manual action. Show the Google rating visibly as content instead ("5.0 on Google, 37 reviews, updated 1 Nov 2026"), with a link to the profile.
  3. If LeadHive later collects **first-party** reviews on SR's own site (a review form on `/reviews`, with name, rating and comment), AggregateRating can describe *those* reviews only. It must exactly match the visible count and average, it must never be curated or hand-compiled, and it still won't show stars. It is only worth doing for completeness.
- Example (truthful, first-party only; values must equal what's on the page):

{
  "@context": "https://schema.org",
  "@type": "HomeAndConstructionBusiness",
  "name": "SR Shutters & Screens",

  "sameAs": ["https://g.page/XXXX", "https://facebook.com/XXXX"],
  "aggregateRating": {
