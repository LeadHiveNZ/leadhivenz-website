# The Lead-Gen Model, Pricing and Tradie Objections — LeadHive Playbook

Distilled from: DP Marketing (the case *against* pay-per-lead, aimed at plumbers/HVAC), James Kuck (rank and rent: selling exclusive leads to local businesses), plus pricing benchmarks from the Google Ads and LSA sources. Full briefs in `sources/`.

## 1. How tradies are being taught to see you (know this objection cold)

DP Marketing's video is made *for plumbers and HVAC owners*. Its argument:
- Pay-per-lead (PPL) = "push a button, get a lead". You own nothing: not the platform, the customer data, or the targeting.
- Four buckets they lump together: **aggregators** (HomeAdvisor/Angi; NZ/AU equivalents are Builderscrack, NoCowboys, hipages, Oneflare, ServiceSeeking), **black-box Facebook/Google lead agencies** (that's you), **rented ranked websites**, and **agencies that keep the data** when you leave.
- The killer line: *"With PPL you never get more efficient. Getting busier costs more, linearly. Owning your marketing makes your cost per lead fall over time."*
- Momentum drops to zero the day they stop paying.

**Pre-empt it in the sales call** (don't wait for them to raise it):
1. **Exclusive, not shared.** "Builderscrack/hipages send the same job to 3–5 tradies and you race to the phone. Every LeadHive lead goes to you only, and you're the only [trade] we work with in [region]."
2. **Speed to revenue vs. years of SEO.** "Owning your marketing is the right long-term move, and we'll help you build it. But SEO takes 6–12 months and thousands up front. We get your phone ringing this month."
3. **Give them ownership of what matters.** Decide your policy and say it plainly: they keep every customer's details, the call recordings and the reviews they earn. (Consider building on the client's own Google Business Profile, and a buy-out or handover clause for the landing page and number.) This kills the "data trap" objection and differentiates you from the agencies DP Marketing warns about.
4. **Price against the alternative.** Google Local Services Ads run ~US$25–$45 per lead and need managing (~US$549–$597/month management + setup). Search clicks for plumbing run ~US$15–$60 with 2–4 clicks per call. Show the client what 15–25 exclusive leads would cost them to buy and manage themselves.

**Retention implication:** the "rented" fear grows over time. Counter it with monthly reports showing calls, answered rate, booked jobs and estimated revenue, plus reviews gained. Clients who see ROI and feel they're building something (reviews, customer list) stay.

## 2. The "send free leads first" close (rank and rent, adapted)

James Kuck's method for selling leads to local businesses:
1. **Research the niche:** job value to the business, and what HomeAdvisor/Thumbtack-type platforms charge per lead. Check who is already **running Google Ads** and who is **paying for sponsored placements** on directories. They have budget and are frustrated with shared leads.
2. **Build the lead source first.** Don't sell before you have leads; the asset is your leverage.
3. **Route 1–2 real leads to a target business for free**, with a call-tracking **whisper**: *"This lead was provided by [LeadHive]."*
4. **Call them after:** *"I'm sure you heard the whisper: 'LeadHive provided this lead.' That's us. Our partner in [area] couldn't take any more jobs and we're getting calls here and there, so we're looking for a new partner. Are you looking for more work?"*
5. Offer an **exclusive monthly arrangement** on autopay. His benchmark: ~10 exclusive calls/month is worth US$500–$750/month in a niche where jobs are worth US$750–$1,500.
6. If they pass, move down the list: businesses on Google pages 2–5, advertisers with broken landing pages (point out the broken page as a value-first opener).

**LeadHive application — this solves two problems at once:**
- **Opening new regions/trades without a client:** run a small test campaign (e.g. $20–$30/day for 2–3 weeks) in a new town for a trade. That tells you real cost per lead and lead volume in small populations *before* you promise a package. That's the data you currently don't have.
- **Closing:** route the test leads (whisper on) to the best-reviewed tradie in that area, then call them. You're no longer selling a promise; you're offering them more of what they just received.
- Keep the price-per-lead math in front of the client: average job value × close rate × leads per month vs. package price.

## 3. Pricing sanity check for LeadHive packages

Use these to defend price and to set internal targets:
- Your cost: ad spend per lead (track by trade and region) + management time. Target ad spend per lead ≤ ~35–40% of the effective per-lead price you charge.
- NZ Starter $1,500 for 15–25 leads = $60–$100 per lead. Growth $2,000 for 25–35 = $57–$80. AU Dominator $3,400 for 40–60 = $57–$85.
- Compare: LSA $25–$45 US per lead but shared ranking, management cost and US-only availability in many categories; aggregators share each lead with several tradies.
- If a trade/region's ad cost per lead pushes margin under target, raise the price, change the package's lead range, or tighten to higher-value services (hot water replacement, switchboards, blocked drains) where job value supports it.

## 4. Lead quality standard (what counts as a lead)

Borrow LSA's dispute rules so the definition is contractual and clear:
- Counts: a genuine service enquiry, in the service area, for a service the client offers, by call (over ~60s, or answered and discussed) or form with a working phone number.
- Doesn't count (replace or credit): spam/robocalls, sales solicitations, out of area, services not offered, duplicates within X days, uncontactable form submissions.
- The client's side of the deal: answer rate. Missed calls are logged and shown in reports.
