### **SUMMARY**
This video features entrepreneur and sales expert Alex Hormozi breaking down his "CLOSER" framework, a high-performance sales scripting process designed to move prospects from a state of indecision to a firm commitment. The teaching is aimed at business owners, sales professionals, and consultants who face prospects that are hesitant to buy or have deep-seated objections. Hormozi emphasizes that sales is not about providing information, but about solving problems and empowering the prospect to make a definitive decision. The brief explores psychological triggers like price anchoring, outcome-based selling, and the three-layered hierarchy of objections (Circumstances, Others, Self).

### **CORE CONTENT**

#### **The Baseline Assumption (00:08)**
The fundamental starting point for any sale is the assumption that the prospect wants what you have. If they are on the call or in the meeting, they have a problem they want solved.

#### **The CLOSER Framework (00:43)**
This acronym represents the step-by-step scripting process Hormozi uses to navigate a sales conversation from initial greeting to final commitment.

*   **C: Clarify Why They Are There (00:45)**
    *   Ask questions like: "What brought you in today?" or "Why’d you take the time to take this call?"
    *   Continue asking until you get beyond surface-level answers (e.g., "I just want information") to the core problem they are trying to solve.
*   **L: Label Them with a Problem (01:00)**
    *   Repeat the problem back to the prospect to ensure they acknowledge it.
    *   Goal: Get the prospect to admit they have a specific struggle.
*   **O: Overview Past Experiences and Pains (01:13)**
    *   Hormozi calls this the "Pain Cycle." Ask what they have done in the past to solve the problem, what worked, and what failed.
    *   Affirm the person throughout this stage to build rapport (01:37).
*   **S: Sell the Vacation (01:52)**
    *   Once the prospect is "in the thick of the pain," ask for permission to tell them about your solution.
    *   Use **3 main points (anecdotes)**. Hormozi notes that people remember "threes" best and it is easier to triangulate ideas (02:09).
    *   Instead of explaining features, tell short stories or analogies to explain concepts.
*   **E: Explain Away Concerns (07:05)**
    *   This is the transition into handling "No." In sales, "No" is where the actual job begins.
    *   Differentiate between **Obstacles** (things that come up before price is presented) and **Objections** (things that come up after price).
*   **R: Reinforce the Decision (08:33)**
    *   Physically walk them through the decision-making process using the "Three Circles of Power" (see below).

#### **The Psychology of Price Anchoring (04:00)**
Hormozi argues against the common advice of "price minimizing" (e.g., "It’s only $100").
*   **The Tactic:** State that the product is "extremely expensive" before revealing the price.
*   **The Logic:** This braces the prospect. When the actual price is revealed, it either matches their braced expectation or is lower than what they imagined, leading to relief (04:55).
*   **The Risk of "Only":** If you say "It's only $200," but the prospect thought it would be $50, you have "ripped their expectation" (04:47).

#### **Outcome-Based Selling vs. Program-Based Selling (05:30)**
*   People do not buy memberships or programs; they buy results.
*   **Example:** In a weight loss context, don’t sell a 12-month gym membership. Sell the "36 pounds" the prospect needs to lose and the 36 weeks it will take to get there (05:58).

#### **Neutral and Negative-Based Closing Questions (06:24)**
Based on Chris Voss’s negotiation techniques, Hormozi prefers questions that allow the prospect to say "No" to a negative or agree to a "Fair" proposition.
*   **Neutral:** "Fair enough?" is easier to agree to than "Does that sound amazing?" (06:33).
*   **Negative:** "Would it be totally crazy if we moved forward today?" (06:51).

#### **The Three Buckets of Objections (The Three Circles) (08:40)**
When a prospect says "No," their reason will always fall into one of three buckets. A salesperson must peel back these layers like an onion.

1.  **Circumstances (09:09):** The prospect blames external factors.
    *   **Time:** "I don't have enough time."
    *   **Money:** "I don't have the funds."
    *   **Fit:** "I don't think this specific program fits my identity" (e.g., "I don't eat broccoli" or "I don't do cardio").
2.  **Other People (12:12):** The prospect blames a spouse, partner, or boss.
    *   **The Pivot:** Shift from "asking for permission" to "asking for support" (12:55).
3.  **Self (Self-Doubt/Avoidance) (15:14):** The prospect is afraid of their own failure.
    *   This is the deepest level. If they aren't blaming money or a spouse, they are likely afraid they won't follow through.

### **SCRIPTS AND COPY**

**On Clarification (00:45):**
*   "What brought you in today?"
*   "Why’d you respond to the ad?"
*   "Why’d you take time to take this call?"

**On Labeling the Problem (01:04):**
*   "So let me get this straight: You’re here because you’re trying to lose weight and you are struggling to do so at current. Is that correct?"

**On Selling the "Vacation" (02:00):**
*   "Given everything you just said, I think you’re going to be perfect for what we have. Can I tell you about it?"

**On Price Anchoring (04:02):**
*   "Well, given X, Y, and Z, I will tell you: this is extremely expensive."

**Closing Questions (06:33 - 06:51):**
*   "Fair enough?"
*   "You wouldn't be opposed to moving forward, would you?"
*   "Would it be totally crazy if we started you on Monday?"

**Handling the Spouse Objection (12:17 - 12:55):**
*   "Are they aware of the fact that you’re struggling with this?"
*   "Do they approve of the struggle?"
*   "If the roles were reversed, would you be supportive of them?"
*   "I think you're asking for permission when you should be asking for support."

**The "Rocking Chair" Close (06:11):**
*   "Let’s be real. You’re not going to go and actually sit on your rocking chair on the balcony and think, 'Hmm, am I going to do this program?' Of course not. You’re going to get in your car, your mom’s going to call you, you’ve got the kids... and five days from now, you’re going to have some moment where you check your bank account and think, 'Shoot, I really should have done that thing.'"

**The "Burn You Twice" Analogy (07:11):**
*   "Imagine if you had your first 6th-grade boyfriend or girlfriend, and after that breakup, you swore off men or women forever. That’d be ridiculous, right? It’s the same thing here. You’re going to let that previous bad experience burn you twice: once when it happened, and now when it’s preventing you from doing what you need to do."

### **WARNINGS**

*   **Avoid "Information Seekers" (00:54):** If a prospect says they just want information, call it out. "You’re not hopping on 20 calls a day just for information, are you? No. So what problem are we solving?"
*   **The Danger of "Only" (04:47):** Using the word "only" when presenting price sets a low expectation. If your price is higher than their "low," you lose the sale instantly.
*   **Address Obstacles Early (07:51):** Resolve as many concerns as possible *before* presenting the price. If you wait until after the price, those small obstacles turn into "scarier monsters of objections."
*   **The Identity Trap (Fit) (10:11):** Prospects often try to keep the variables that caused their problem (e.g., "I want to lose weight but keep my current breakfast"). You must tell them, "That breakfast got you that body; we have to change the change."
