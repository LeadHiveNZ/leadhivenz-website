### **2. SUMMARY**
This video provides a specific, tactical script for digital agencies to navigate the "discovery call"—the first meeting after a prospect has agreed to talk. Alex Berman teaches a framework designed to transition a cold lead into a warm prospect who is eager for a formal proposal. The method relies heavily on case studies and result-oriented questioning to qualify the lead and establish authority. It is designed for agency owners and B2B sales professionals looking to streamline their sales process and close enterprise-level deals.

### **3. CORE CONTENT**

#### **The Agency Sales Process Context (00:18)**
Berman outlines the typical steps to a sale:
1.  **Initial Meeting:** The Discovery Call (The focus of this video).
2.  **Proposal:** Moving from the call to a written plan.
3.  **Contract:** Finalizing the legal terms.
4.  **Sale:** Closing the deal.

#### **The Discovery Call Framework**

**Step 1: Introductions and Small Talk (04:44)**
*   Keep this section under 2 minutes.
*   Goal: Establish basic rapport without wasting time.

**Step 2: Present the Case Study (01:07)**
Instead of a general pitch, start with a specific story of what you did for a similar client. This should take 1-2 minutes. Hit these three points (01:36):
1.  **The Client’s Problem:** What were they struggling with?
2.  **The Solution:** What specific service or software did your agency build/provide?
3.  **The Results:** What was the ROI? What happened after the solution was implemented?

**Step 3: The Problem Identification Question (01:57)**
Immediately follow the case study with a qualifying question: *"Have you struggled with similar issues when it comes to <issue>?"*
*   **If YES (02:25):** Move directly to pitching your process and asking for a proposal.
*   **If NO (03:20):** Transition to "Goal Identification."

**Step 4: Goal Identification (The "No" Path) (03:30)**
If the prospect doesn't identify with your first case study, ask an open-ended question about their objectives: *"What are your goals for 2017 [or current year]?"*
*   Take notes on their goals.
*   Identify a goal that can be solved with software or your agency's specific services.
*   **Re-cycle the Case Study:** Once a relevant goal is identified, tell a *second* case study that matches that specific goal (04:07).
*   Follow the three-point structure again: Client Problem, Your Solution, The Results.

**Step 5: The Proposal Ask (03:00, 04:54)**
Once a pain point or goal is identified, ask for permission to create a proposal. Do not just send it; get their verbal "yes" to the idea.

**Step 6: Setting the Follow-Up (05:04)**
Once they agree to a proposal, set a specific time *during the current call* to run through that proposal. Do not leave it to "coordinate over email."

**Step 7: The Budget "Ballpark" Qualification (05:18)**
Wait until the very end of the call, after they are psychologically invested and have agreed to a proposal meeting, to mention the price range.

### **4. SCRIPTS AND COPY**

**Introduction (04:44):**
*   "Great to talk to you today."
*   "How’s the weather in [City]?"

**The Problem Check (01:57):**
*   "Have you struggled with similar issues when it comes to [Issue]?"

**The Goal Digger (03:30):**
*   "What are your goals for 2017 for the [Department Name] department?"

**The Process Transition (02:25):**
*   "Would you be open to hearing more about our process and how we could do similar for [Prospect's Company Name]?"

**The Case Study Pivot (04:07):**
*   "[Goal/Issue] was a similar issue that [Other Client Name] was having. That’s another one of our clients, and we did..."

**The Proposal Ask (04:54):**
*   "I’d love to take this back to the [Agency Name] team and come up with a proposal for how we could fix [xyz] issue, would you be open to that?"

**The Appointment Setting (05:04):**
*   "Would you mind setting a time now to run through it?"

**The Budget Qualifier (05:20):**
*   "Hey, before we go—typically these type of projects run between 150-250k. Does that ballpark work for your technology budget?"

---

### **5. WARNINGS**
*   **Avoid Presentations/Decks (01:28, 02:49):** Berman explicitly warns *not* to use a slide deck or "whip out a deck" during the initial discovery call. Decks "bog you down." Only use them if the client specifically asks or if you are at the process presentation stage later in the call.
*   **Limit Small Talk (00:44):** Keep introductions and small talk under 2 minutes to maintain a professional, high-value tone.
*   **Timing the Budget Talk (05:33):** Do not talk about budget at the start of the call. Wait until the end so they are "super invested in the conversation" before the price is introduced.
