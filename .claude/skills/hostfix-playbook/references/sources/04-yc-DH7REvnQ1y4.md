**2. SUMMARY**
In this guide, Tom Blomfield (General Partner at Y Combinator and founder of Monzo and GoCardless) outlines the evolutionary stages of a B2B sales process. The video is designed for early-stage founders who are navigating B2B sales for the first time. It teaches how to transition from low-commitment "design partnerships" to sophisticated recurring revenue contracts. The core philosophy is to move as quickly as possible toward contractual commitment by focusing on narrow "wedge" problems rather than broad platforms, ensuring customers have "skin in the game" through paid trials and clear success metrics.

**3. CORE CONTENT**

**The Progression Framework (00:21)**
B2B sales typically follow a five-stage progression. Founders often get stuck in early stages and must push to reach Stage 4 as fast as possible.

**Stage 1: Design Partnerships (01:39)**
*   **Definition:** Often involves Figma mockups or ideas with little to no code.
*   **The Tactic:** Spend time in the customer's office observing their workflow. Identify "narrow pieces of work" you can automate (03:07).
*   **The "Wedge" Strategy (03:45):** Identify a narrow, burning problem. Build a solution in as little as 48 hours and bring it back to the customer.
*   **Social Proof:** Use the partner’s logo on your website to build credibility while iterating toward a product people will pay for.

**Stage 2: Free Trials, Pilots, or Proof of Concepts (POCs) (06:15)**
*   **The Goal:** Prove value when you lack a long track record.
*   **Success Metrics (07:12):** You must define what you are trying to prove. Use the "Value Equation."
*   **The Value Equation Example (07:38):**
    *   Claim: Solving 20% of inbound customer service queries.
    *   Impact: Reducing a team from 100 to 80 employees.
    *   Financial Value: $1,000,000 in salaries saved per year.
    *   Pricing: Charge $200,000 for the software (approx. 20% of the value delivered).
*   **Pilot Mechanics (08:38):** Use back-testing on historical data or side-by-side trials with existing processes to show that the AI/Software provides better results than current human agents.

**Stage 3: Paid Trials (10:04)**
*   **The Logic:** Paying makes customers more serious about the product.
*   **The Shortcut (10:45):** Ask for the amount the "Champion" can personally approve (e.g., $10,000 - $20,000 on a corporate credit card) to bypass a full procurement process.
*   **Pilot Conditions (11:04):** Don't start until the customer has a "live project" or real data ready to test. Schedule check-ins every few days.
*   **Timeframe (12:01):** Keep pilots as short as possible: 7 to 14 days.

**Stage 4: Recurring Revenue Contracts (The "Pro Move") (11:39)**
*   **Mechanism:** An annual or monthly recurring contract with a 30-to-60-day "opt-out" or money-back guarantee period at the start.
*   **The Benefit:** If the customer does nothing, it automatically becomes a full recurring contract. This avoids having to run a second sales process after a pilot ends.

**Stage 5: Customer Success (13:36)**
*   **Implementation Gap:** Blomfield cites a company with $4M in signed contracts but less than $2M implemented.
*   **Strategy:** Dedicate significant effort to onboarding to ensure the customer actually uses the software and sees the promised ROI.

**Bonus Tips for Closing (13:58)**
*   **Security (14:05):** Get SOC2, HIPAA, or ISO 27001 certifications started immediately; they can delay deals by months.
*   **The Champion (14:25):** Treat your internal contact like a co-founder. They are the person who sells for you when you aren't in the room.
*   **Defining the Buying Process (14:48):** Map out the "Economic Buyer," "Technical Approver," "Security Gatekeeper," "Legal Team," and "Day-to-Day Users."
*   **In-Person Visits (15:20):** Physically visit the customer to move contracts along faster.
*   **Scarcity (16:12):** Limit capacity (e.g., "We can only take on two new enterprise customers this quarter") to create urgency.

**4. SCRIPTS AND COPY**

**Discovery and Ideation Scripts:**
*   "What's the part of your job you hate the most?" (03:16)
*   "If you could wave a magic wand, what part of your work would you get rid of?" (03:18)

**Pilot Engagement Scripts:**
*   "Give us a sample of a thousand queries and let's measure how many we can actually solve for you." (08:08)
*   "If I can solve this problem for you and deliver these metrics we've talked about, how much would that be worth to you?" (09:48)

**Closing and Logistics Scripts:**
*   "Describe the last time you bought software like this. Who internally had to approve it? What was the process?" (14:51)
*   "I'm going to be in Houston next week. What do you say about getting lunch?" (15:29)
*   "We're talking with seven or eight potential customers, but we really only have the capacity to work with two enterprise customers this quarter. If you're interested, we'd love to get a commitment; otherwise, let's talk again in six months." (16:14)

**5. WARNINGS**
*   **The Design Partnership Trap (02:18):** These partnerships are often too long (3–6 months), poorly defined in scope, and suffer from low engagement because the customer has no "skin in the game."
*   **The "Unpaid Dev Shop" (05:13):** Do not let a customer give you a massive list of requirements specific only to their business for free. Avoid bespoke work without a path to a scalable product.
*   **The "Pre-Proof of Concept" (06:42):** Beware of customers asking for "pre-POCs" or other low-commitment steps that don't lead to actual sales.
*   **API Integration Delay (12:50):** Do not do a full API integration during a pilot if it requires the customer's engineering time. Use CSV/Excel imports/exports to show value immediately without a months-long technical wait.
*   **The Legal/NDA Loop (15:36):** Do not get caught in multiple rounds of redlining contracts or NDAs. Be flexible on minor clauses unless they are "company-ending" (like transferring all IP to the customer).
*   **Reporting MRR (13:08):** Be careful reporting revenue to investors as MRR/ARR if the customer is still in an opt-out or guarantee period. Be transparent about where the customer sits in the cycle.

**6. CLAIMS TO VERIFY**
*   **48-Hour Turnaround (03:51):** Claim that a narrow wedge product can be built in 48 hours.
*   **Financial ROI Example (07:38):** Example of an AI tool saving 20% of labor costs, resulting in $1M savings, justifying a $200k price tag.
*   **SOC2 Speed (14:05):** Recommends companies like Vanta to expedite security certifications.
*   **Opt-out Conversion:** The claim that Stage 4 contracts (opt-out) act "like magic" to convert pilots into recurring revenue without a second sales hurdle.
