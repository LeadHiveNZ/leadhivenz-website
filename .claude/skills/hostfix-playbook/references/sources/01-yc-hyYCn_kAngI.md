### **2. SUMMARY**
This session teaches early-stage startup founders how to transition from user research to acquiring their first paying customers. The core philosophy is built on "doing things that don't scale," emphasizing that founders must manually recruit customers rather than relying on automated marketing. Gustaf Alströmer provides a framework for founder-led sales, including how to write cold outreach emails, manage a sales funnel, set pricing, and calculate the volume of outreach required to hit growth goals.

### **3. CORE CONTENT**

#### **The Mindset: Doing Things That Don't Scale (00:44)**
*   **The Paul Graham Principle:** Startups take off because founders *make* them take off. Growth does not take care of itself.
*   **Manual Recruitment:** Founders must manually recruit users. You cannot wait for users to come to you; you must go out and get them.
*   **The "Coding Trap":** Avoid the common mistake of believing you can solve a lack of users by writing more code or building more features (01:45).

#### **The Startup Curve (02:17)**
Most startups follow a specific trajectory:
1.  **Launch:** Often via Product Hunt, Hacker News, or TechCrunch.
2.  **Wearing Off of Novelty:** Initial excitement fades.
3.  **Trough of Sorrow:** A long period where growth is stagnant or difficult. Many companies die here because they give up or move too slowly.
4.  **Releases of Improvement:** Listening to users and iterating.
5.  **Wiggles of False Hope:** Small bumps that don't lead to scale.
6.  **The Promised Land:** Achieving Product-Market Fit.

#### **Founder-Led Sales (03:43)**
*   **Why Founders Must Sell:** You need to know your customers intimately. Sales and user interviews are two sides of the same coin.
*   **Control Over Destiny:** You cannot outsource engineering in the early days; similarly, you cannot outsource sales.
*   **Authority:** As the founder, you know the problem, the product, and the market better than any hired salesperson. This makes you an expert in the customer's eyes (04:35).
*   **Hiring Rule:** Do not hire a sales team until you know how to do sales yourself (04:15).

#### **The Sales Funnel: Founder Speak vs. Sales Speak (08:37)**
| Stage in Founder Speak | Stage in Sales Speak |
| :--- | :--- |
| Make a list of potential customers | Prospecting / Lead Generation |
| Email or message them | Qualification |
| Schedule and run meetings | Sales calls |
| Disclose price and close them | Proposal made |
| Revenue generated | Sale |

*   **The Missing Step:** Onboarding. After the sale, you must manually ensure the customer successfully uses the product to avoid churn (09:21).

#### **Targeting Your First Customers (09:48)**
*   **Data Tracking:** Use a spreadsheet or simple CRM with columns for Industry, Company, Title, Name, Email, and LinkedIn URI.
*   **Prioritization Strategy:** Your first customers should be your **easiest** customers (10:43).
    *   Sell to your personal network.
    *   Sell to other startups (they have less bureaucracy and shorter decision-making lines).
    *   Avoid the "95%": Most people are not early adopters. Don't waste time trying to convince someone who isn't interested in trying new products (12:33).

#### **The Numbers Game: Working Backwards (15:31)**
To get 2 customers, you cannot start with 10 leads. You must calculate based on conversion drop-offs.
*   **Hypothetical Funnel Benchmarks (16:05):**
    *   **Outreach:** 500 emails/messages.
    *   **Open Rate (50%):** 250 opens.
    *   **Reply Rate (5%):** 20 replies.
    *   **Demo Conversion (50%):** 10 demos.
    *   **Customer Conversion (20%):** 2 customers.

#### **Initial Sales Motions by Company (18:42)**
*   **Amplitude:** Outbound sales emails.
*   **Dropbox:** Hacker News / Product-led.
*   **Front:** Outbound sales to very small companies.
*   **Stripe:** Product-led and outbound sales to engineers.
*   **Figma:** Product-led targeting designers.
*   **Gusto:** Product-led targeting founders at startups.

### **4. SCRIPTS AND COPY**

#### **The "Great Sales Email" Template (06:42)**
1.  **Length:** 6–8 sentences maximum.
2.  **Language:** Plain text, no HTML, no jargon, no buzzwords.
3.  **Authority:** State clearly that you are the founder.
4.  **Social Proof:** Briefly mention impressive previous work or existing customers.
5.  **Call to Action:** Ask for a specific call or meeting (e.g., a Calendly link).

#### **Verbatim Email Examples**

**Brex Early Outreach Email (05:55):**
> "Hey guys! We’re opening up our beta for our W17 batch friends with 10 spots for beta users!... Brex is a corporate credit card focused on technology companies... We don’t require a personal guarantee and we can give you the highest limits... How much does it cost? It’s free. The merchant is paying us so there are zero annual fees."

**BrightReps Outreach Example (19:29):**
> "Hi Ben, It’s nice to meet you over email. I’ve been chatting with Michael Shan about our company, BrightReps, and he suggested I reach out to you. We just released an app on Salesforce that’s designed to simplify processes and save time for customer support teams. I’d love to give you a demo sometime and get your feedback. Are there any dates that would work well for you after the holidays?"

**Laudable Outreach Example (19:42):**
> "Hi Nicolas, I’m hoping to connect with [you]... Recruiting videos - saw this AI Product Development team: team recruiting clip and am wondering if you’re considering a broader employee spotlight campaign?... For context, Laudable makes it easy to create 4k video content remotely..."

### **5. WARNINGS**
*   **The "Trough of Sorrow" Trap:** Founders often give up during the slow period between launch and product-market fit (02:54).
*   **Hiring Too Early:** Do not hire a salesperson to "figure out" sales for you. If you don't know how to sell it, they won't either (04:15).
*   **The Qualification Failure:** Do not be afraid to let a potential customer go if they are moving too slowly. If they aren't ready to buy after 2-3 calls, tell them to check back in 6 months (11:25).
*   **The "Free Trial" Mistake:** In B2B, offer a money-back guarantee or an opt-out from an annual contract instead of a completely free trial. Paid pilots are better than unpaid pilots because payment is the only real proof of value (14:40).
*   **The Data Illusion:** Founders often send 100 emails, get 0 customers, and conclude "sales doesn't work." They usually just haven't sent enough emails to find the early adopters (17:05).

### **6. CLAIMS TO VERIFY**
*   **Brex Result:** Claimed their first 10 customers came directly from their own YC batch using manual outreach (05:14).
*   **Pricing Strategy:** Claimed you should increase your price until customers are complaining but still paying (15:18).
*   **Outreach Success:** An example email shown for "Live Deploy" (19:47) reportedly landed 22 customers from a single outreach campaign.
