# Knowledge Brief: How to Get Your First 100 Customers

**Header**
*   **Title:** How to Get Your First 100 Customers for Your SaaS Product
*   **Creator/Channel:** Rob Walling / MicroConf

*   **Upload Date:** Approximately late 2022/early 2023 (based on video content references)

---

**SUMMARY**
In this video, serial entrepreneur and investor Rob Walling provides an actionable roadmap for SaaS founders to secure their first 100 customers. The core philosophy centers on "marketing before coding"—validating demand through landing pages and direct conversations long before a product is finished. Walling divides the journey into three distinct phases: seeking unfair advantages (audience and network), "scratching and clawing" through unscalable manual tactics, and finally building a sustainable marketing flywheel. This guide is specifically for first-time or early-stage SaaS founders who need to transition from an idea to a revenue-generating business.

---

**CORE CONTENT**

### Phase 1: Pre-Validation (Before Writing Code)
Walling emphasizes that the search for the first 100 customers begins before a single line of code is written (00:35).

*   **Look for Advantages (00:40):** Evaluate if you have an existing audience or a network in a specific niche. 
    *   **Audience:** Do you have people who already listen to you?
    *   **Network:** Do you have peers or influencers you can call upon for webinars, co-promotions, or introductions?
*   **Audience vs. SaaS (01:05):** While audiences are essential for info-products and courses, Walling notes they are not strictly necessary for SaaS. However, building a **network** of other founders is non-negotiable for support and distribution. Recommended communities include (01:45):
    *   Indie Hackers
    *   MicroConf Connect (Slack community)
    *   The Dynamite Circle
*   **Marketing Before Coding (02:18):** Building a product for 6–12 months in a "basement" without marketing is a recipe for failure. 
*   **The Landing Page Strategy (02:45):** 
    *   Create a landing page to validate interest and need.
    *   Capture emails by offering updates or early access.
    *   **Customer Development (03:30):** Once you have 100–500 emails, reach out to 50 of them for "sanity checks" on features and pricing.

### Phase 2: Validating Interest and Public Launch
*   **Public Dialogue (03:53):** Talk about your idea in public early. Walling shares that for his last SaaS app, he secured 11 "yeses" (commitments to pay) before writing code (04:02).
*   **Execution over Ideas (04:15):** Don't fear "idea theft." Success is 1% idea and 99% execution.
*   **The Launch Sequence (04:55):**
    *   **2-Week Lead Time:** Notify your email list two weeks before the launch.
    *   **Teasers:** Use mock-ups, screenshots, and short videos to build anticipation.
    *   **Early Incentives (05:08):** Offer exclusive benefits to your email list to convert them early. Tactics include:
        *   Free onboarding.
        *   Free migration from a competitor’s tool.
        *   Launch discounts (though Walling prefers value-adds over price cuts).

### Phase 3: "Scratching and Clawing" (Doing Things That Don't Scale)
Walling references Paul Graham’s "do things that don’t scale" (05:40). These are one-time events that get you to the first 100 but won't necessarily get you to 1,000.

*   **Manual Channels (05:58):**
    *   **Product Hunt:** Use a structured launch (referencing Derrick Reimer's SavvyCal launch as a benchmark).
    *   **Hacker News & Reddit:** Direct engagement in relevant subreddits or "Show HN."
    *   **Quora & Stack Exchange:** Walling cites a founder who reached $35k MRR primarily through answering questions on these platforms (06:10).
    *   **AppSumo Deals:** For immediate (though often one-time) cash and user influx.
    *   **Podcast Tours:** Guesting on niche shows to reach your target demographic.

### Phase 4: Pricing and The Marketing Flywheel
*   **The Pricing Trap (07:05):** Avoid underpricing. 
    *   **$5/month:** 100 customers only equals $500 MRR. This is not enough to live on or reinvest.
    *   **$100/month:** 100 customers equals $10k MRR, allowing you to quit your day job.
*   **Flywheel Strategy (07:55):** To move past the first 100, you must pick one of the **6 Most Common SaaS Marketing Approaches** (08:24):
    1.  **SEO:** Long-term organic search.
    2.  **Content Marketing:** Building authority.
    3.  **Cold Outreach:** Directly emailing/calling prospects.
    4.  **Partnerships:** Co-marketing with non-competing tools.
    5.  **Integrations:** Building on platforms like Shopify, Slack, or Salesforce.
    6.  **PPC Ads:** Google/Facebook ads (only viable if your price point is high enough).

### The Retention Factor (09:17)
Walling warns that finding 100 customers is only half the battle. If you acquire 10 customers but "churn" (lose) 10 in the same month, your growth is flat. Retaining customers is often a harder challenge than acquiring them.

**SCRIPTS AND COPY**

*   **Pricing Sanity Check (03:40):** "Does this [mock-up] solve the problem I think it solves? Does this provide value for you? Would you pay $30, $50, or $100 a month for this product?"
*   **Landing Page Value Prop (03:15):** "This is what we’re building. If you’re interested in this and the value it provides, enter your email and we’ll be in touch."
*   **The "Yes" Commitment (04:02):** Use social media, podcasts, and forums to ask: "I'm building [X] to solve [Y]. If I build it, would you be willing to be an early paid user?"

---

**WARNINGS**

*   **The Coding Trap (02:22):** Do not spend 6–12 months building a product in isolation. You must market while you build.
*   **Underpricing (07:03):** Pricing at $5 or $10 a month makes it nearly impossible to afford paid acquisition or reach a "living wage" MRR with your first 100 customers.
*   **The Churn Trap (09:20):** High acquisition means nothing if retention is low. Walling notes it can take 6, 12, or even 18 months to build a product that people actually stay with long-term.
*   **Channel Fatigue (05:50):** Things like Product Hunt and Reddit launches are "one-time events." They provide spikes, not sustainable growth.
