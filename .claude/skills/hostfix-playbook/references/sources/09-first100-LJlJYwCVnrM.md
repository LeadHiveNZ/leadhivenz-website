### **2. SUMMARY**
This video provides a tactical, three-step Go-To-Market (GTM) strategy for early-stage startup founders who have finished a product but have no active users. Oskar Bader argues that most founders fail because they engage in "random acts of marketing"—posting on social media and running ads without a clear plan. The video teaches a transition from a product-centric mindset to a problem-centric one, emphasizing the importance of solving high-intensity "painkiller" problems for a hyper-specific audience. The core methodology involves conducting non-sales interviews to "steal" the language of potential customers, which is then used to create high-converting marketing copy and valid product offers.

### **3. CORE CONTENT**

#### **Introduction and the "Founder Trap" (00:00 - 01:00)**
Many founders believe that once a product is finished, the next logical step is to run ads or send cold emails. Bader shares his own failure: after six months of random posting and ads, he still had zero users because he lacked a specific plan and a defined target audience.

#### **Step 1: Identify the "Big Problem" (01:00 - 02:45)**
Founders often get lost in "feature creep" (onboarding flows, dark mode, integrations) and forget the core problem they are solving.
*   **The Specificity Rule:** Avoid generic claims like "saves time" or "makes money." You must be specific, such as "saving two hours a week by automating weekly reports."
*   **Painkiller vs. Vitamin (02:05):** Nobody rushes out at 11:00 PM to buy a vitamin, but they will for a painkiller. Your product must address an urgent need that causes immediate friction for the user.
*   **The Foundation:** This specific problem becomes the core message for your website, ads, and content.

#### **Step 2: Define the Hyper-Specific User (02:45 - 04:30)**
The biggest mistake is claiming "everybody can use this." When you build for everyone, you build for no one, and your marketing becomes a series of guesses.
*   **The "Stranger in a Room" Test (03:30):** You must describe your ideal user so specifically that a stranger could pick them out of a crowded room.
*   **Example Profiling (Meditation App):**
    *   **Generic:** People who are stressed.
    *   **Hyper-Specific:** Office workers between 25–40 years old who have back-to-back meetings all day, feel chronically stressed, and are currently Googling "how to relax at work."
*   **Outcome:** Specificity allows you to know exactly where to find them (e.g., LinkedIn vs. Instagram) and what to charge them.

#### **Step 3: The 10-User Interview Process (04:30 - 07:50)**
Before running any paid ads, you must talk to 10 "Real People" who fit your hyper-specific profile.
*   **The Goal:** Do not pitch. Your goal is to hear the **exact words and phrases** they use to describe their frustration.
*   **The Outreach:** Send a low-pressure message asking for a 15-minute feedback call.
*   **The Interview Flow (05:40):** 
    *   Let them talk while you take notes.
    *   Focus on how they handle the problem today and what they’ve tried before.
*   **The Demo (06:05):** If the product is a fit, ask to show a 5-minute demo. Show them exactly how the tool solves the specific frustrations they just mentioned.

#### **The Conversion and Iteration (07:50 - 09:15)**
*   **The "Early User" Offer:** Transition from the demo to a "lifetime" discount offer. 
*   **Learning from Rejection:** If users say "no," it doesn't mean you failed; it means you either spoke to the wrong people or the problem isn't painful enough. Go back to Step 1 and sharpen the "Painkiller" profile.
*   **Scaling through Copy (08:00):** Take the exact phrases from the calls and replace your generic headlines. 
    *   **Before:** "Meditation made simple."
    *   **After:** "The meditation app for people who feel guilty taking a break."

### **4. SCRIPTS AND COPY**

#### **Outreach Script (05:00)**
> "Hey [First Name], I’m talking to [Target Audience] who [Problem]. I want to learn how you handle [Task] today. What works and what’s frustrating. Would you be open to a quick 15-min call this or next week?"

#### **Specific Outreach Example (05:15)**
> "Hey Sarah, I'm speaking with people who spend most of their day in meetings and often feel stressed at work. I'd love to learn how you manage that today and what's most frustrating about it. Would you be open to a quick 15-minute chat this or next week?"

#### **Interview Questions (05:50)**
*   "How are you handling this problem today?"
*   "What’s most frustrating about it?"
*   "What have you tried before to fix it?"

#### **Transition to Demo (06:05)**
> "We’ve been working on solving this exact problem. Can I quickly show you what we’ve built?"

#### **The Pricing Offer Script (06:40)**
> "I’ll release this for €20 a month later, but as an early user, I would like to offer it to you for €10 a month, lifetime. What do you think?"

#### **Headline Evolution Example (08:10)**
*   **User Quote:** "I felt guilty stepping away for a break, so I actually never relax."
*   **New Website Headline:** "The meditation app for people who feel guilty taking a break."

### **5. WARNINGS**
*   **The "Vitamin" Trap:** Do not build something that is "nice to have." If the user isn't actively seeking a solution or feeling the pain, they won't pay.
*   **The "Everyone" Fallacy:** Avoid broad targeting. Broad targeting leads to "random" headlines and guesswork on pricing and channels.
*   **Premature Scaling:** Do not spend money on ads until you have had at least 10 real-world conversations.
*   **Over-Pitching:** Do not spend the interview talking about your features. If you talk more than the prospect, you are learning nothing.
*   **Generic Metrics:** Don't settle for "Saves time" or "Makes more money." If you can't be specific about the problem, you can't be specific about the value.
