# Launch & First Customers

Sources: Michael Seibel (How to Plan an MVP), Paul Graham (do things that don't scale), Gustaf Alströmer (How to Get Your First Customers), Rob Walling / MicroConf (first 100 SaaS customers), Oskar Bader (first 10 paying users), Supademo / Starter Story (first 100 users). Briefs in `sources/01-…`, `02-…`, `03-…`, `07-…`, `08-…`, `09-…`.

## 1. What "launch" means
- Seibel: "Launch something bad quickly." Launch = the day you start getting customers, not a press event. Nobody remembers Google's launch day.
- Airbnb's first version had no payments and no map. Stripe's founders went to customers' offices and installed it by hand.
- Hold the problem and the customer tightly; hold the solution loosely. Iterate (same customer, same problem, better solution) before pivoting.
- Time-box it: pick a date (e.g. 3 weeks), write the feature list down, and **cut features rather than move the date**.

**HostFix application**
- Don't make founding owners wait for the app. Run a **text-based concierge service now**: the owner or cleaner texts a photo and description; Joe quotes the fixed price, dispatches the tradie, and texts updates at each step. Log every job in a sheet.
- That sheet becomes the app spec. Build only what the manual jobs prove people need.
- Change the ad and landing page wording from "be notified when the app launches" to "Founding properties are live now. Log your first job by text."

## 2. Do things that don't scale (Paul Graham)
Handmade, artisanal, painstaking work for early customers. "If you don't do them, you'll never be big anyway." Examples for HostFix:
- Visit each founding property and record the lockbox, water mains, switchboard, hot water cylinder and photos yourself.
- Personally check the first 20 completed jobs (photos, owner feedback call).
- Ring owners after each job: "Was the price fair? Was the tradie on time? What else breaks in this place?"

## 3. First customers come from the easiest place (Gustaf Alströmer, YC)
- Founders do the selling. Don't hire sales until you know how to sell it.
- Start with the easiest customers: your network and warm inbound. Skip the 95% who aren't early adopters.
- Founder speak vs. sales funnel: list of prospects → message → meeting → price and close → **onboarding** (the step founders forget; churn happens here).
- Work backwards from the numbers. Cold outreach example: 500 messages → 250 opened → 20 replies → 10 demos → 2 customers. Sending 100 and quitting proves nothing.
- Don't chase slow prospects. After 2–3 calls, tell them to check back in 6 months.
- Use paid pilots or a money-back guarantee, not free trials. "Raise the price until people complain but still pay."

## 4. Waitlist to paid (Rob Walling, MicroConf)
- Market before (and while) you build. A landing page plus email capture; then contact 50 of the list for sanity checks on features and price.
- Get commitments before building: Walling had 11 paid "yeses" before writing code.
- Launch sequence: tell the list 2 weeks ahead, tease with screenshots and videos, and give early-adopter incentives. He prefers value-adds (free onboarding, free setup) over discounts.
- Don't underprice: at $5/month, 100 customers is $500 a month. Price for a business that can fund growth.
- Pick one long-term channel after the first 100: SEO, content, cold outreach, partnerships, integrations or paid ads.
- Churn kills growth: 10 new and 10 lost a month is flat.

## 5. Painkiller, specific customer, their words (Oskar Bader)
- Painkiller, not vitamin: something people rush to fix at 11pm. A leaking shower before a guest arrives is a painkiller.
- Describe the customer so precisely a stranger could pick them out of a room. HostFix example: "Queenstown owner with 1–3 Airbnbs who lives elsewhere or works full-time, and currently rings round tradies or asks the cleaner to sort it."
- Talk to 10 of them before spending on ads. Ask "How do you handle repairs today? What's most frustrating? What have you tried?"
- Steal their exact phrases for headlines. Example: a lead note says an owner "rings round for tradies himself now", which gives a hook like "Stop ringing round for tradies."
- Outreach script: "Hey [Name], I'm talking to Queenstown Airbnb owners about how they handle repairs between guests: what works and what's frustrating. Open to a quick 15-min call this week?"
- Early-user offer: "It'll be $X/month later; as a founding owner it's half that, locked in. What do you think?"

## 6. Everywhere at once (Supademo)
- No single magic channel. Be everywhere your users are: search, word of mouth, building in public.
- "Competitor alternative" pages capture people already searching (e.g. "Turno maintenance alternative NZ", "Airbnb handyman Queenstown").
- Give value first in communities: answer maintenance questions in host Facebook groups with genuinely useful help.
- "If you're not embarrassed by the first version, you launched too late." Speed is a small company's only advantage over incumbents.
